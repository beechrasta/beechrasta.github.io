The 9 Things ChatGPT Catching Google in Search Proves in 2026, and Why the 20 Percent Number Is Not the Whole Story

Google still owns search. ChatGPT just stopped asking for permission to compete with it.

OpenAI's rise, from chatbot to a second search habit. Credit: Search Engine Roundtable.

Let's stop pretending the search war is still a rumour. OpenAI now says ChatGPT's search-like queries run at roughly one fifth of Google's query volume.

That number comes from OpenAI itself, with no published method behind it. Which is exactly why it is worth pulling apart instead of repeating.

This article is not a victory lap for chatbots. It is about what changes when one in five searches, by OpenAI's count, never touches a results page at all.

1. The claim, stated plainly

  • At an OpenAI dinner in New York on November 2, ChatGPT consumer product lead Adam Fry told Big Technology's Alex Kantrowitz that ChatGPT's volume of search-like queries is now roughly one fifth of Google's.
  • Fry defined a search simply: every time you ask a question and ChatGPT goes and searches the web, that counts.
  • He also said ChatGPT has reached 1.2 billion weekly users, up from 1 billion in about two months.

The so what: even as a company claim, this is the first time OpenAI has put a Google-sized number on the shift.

(Source: Search Engine Roundtable)

2. What counts as a search is doing most of the work

  • Early ChatGPT use was mostly writing and coding help, tasks people do not typically search for.
  • Over the past year, Fry said, users moved into people, places, products, finance, and health questions, the stuff that needs fresh, factual information.
  • Each question that triggers a web search counts separately, not each conversation.

The so what: the definition is generous, and generosity is the whole ballgame here.

(Source: Free Press Journal)

ChatGPT, now a daily lookup habit for 1.2 billion weekly users by OpenAI's count. Credit: AndroidHeadlines.

3. Independent estimates are lower, and that gap matters

  • seoClarity estimated in October 2026 that ChatGPT's daily search activity is about 7.5 percent of Google's.
  • Ahrefs, using mid-2025 data, put ChatGPT at about 12 percent of Google's search volume for things people traditionally searched for.
  • Neither matches OpenAI's 20 percent, because neither uses OpenAI's definition or OpenAI's unpublished Google figure.

The so what: the honest range is somewhere between one in thirteen and one in five, and both ends are already huge.

(Source: Steven Digital)

4. Google is not shrinking, the pie is growing

  • Google itself has said its search service handles more than 5 trillion searches a year.
  • Cloudflare Radar data for the third quarter of 2026 put Google at 89.04 percent of search referrals, up from the previous quarter.
  • Google's query volume is at an all-time high even as chatbots grow, which kills the simple story that one side must be losing for the other to win.

The so what: ChatGPT is mostly capturing new behaviour, not stealing Google's old behaviour one search at a time.

(Source: TechnologyChecker)

5. The traffic story is far more brutal than the query story

  • ChatGPT sending a query is not the same as ChatGPT sending a visitor to your site.
  • Ahrefs found in February 2026 that Google made up nearly 40 percent of traffic to websites while ChatGPT made up 0.21 percent, roughly 190 times less.
  • Across the web, AI platforms sent just 0.32 percent of all website traffic in 2026, up about 16 times since 2024 but still tiny next to organic search at 42.75 percent.

The so what: chatbots answer the question, and the website that used to get the click gets nothing.

(Source: GeoAura)

6. Google saw this coming and answered inside its own results

  • When an AI Overview appears, the top ranking page's click rate drops by about 58 percent, according to Ahrefs data on 300,000 keywords.
  • Searches producing at least one click fell 22.9 percent in relative terms between 2024 and 2026, per SparkToro.
  • Google is now competing with ChatGPT by being a little bit like ChatGPT, answering directly on the results page.

The so what: publishers are being squeezed by both sides, the chatbot that never sends them and the search engine that now answers first.

(Source: BrandChanakya SEO statistics)

Google search, still the default, now answering more questions itself. Credit: Oasis Everywhere Store.

7. Voice and images are the quiet second front

  • Fry said 250 million people a week use ChatGPT's voice input or voice conversations.
  • Users create 1.5 billion images a week inside ChatGPT, by OpenAI's count.
  • OpenAI also plans to merge ChatGPT's Chat and Work modes, removing the toggle at the top of the app.

The so what: search is no longer something you type into a box, and that suits a chatbot far better than a list of links.

(Source: Free Press Journal)

8. The India angle nobody is talking about

  • Google holds about 96.63 percent of search engine share in India, per StatCounter data for September 2026, even higher than its global share.
  • That means the chatbot search shift hits Indian readers differently: Google is even more of a default here, so the habit change has further to travel.
  • But ChatGPT's voice numbers matter more in a country where millions of first time internet users speak before they type.

The so what: in India, voice-first AI search could leapfrog the typed query entirely, and Google's dominance here is no guarantee.

(Source: BrandChanakya SEO statistics)

9. What to do if you make things people search for

  • Stop optimising only for ten blue links, and start optimising for being the answer a chatbot cites.
  • ChatGPT holds about three quarters of AI referral traffic, so one platform's citation habits now shape who gets discovered.
  • Build direct relationships with readers, email, apps, communities, because referral traffic from any search surface is getting thinner.

The so what: the new SEO is not ranking first, it is being quotable by a machine that may never send anyone your way.

(Source: GeoAura)

How to start

  • If you are a writer or publisher: check which of your pages get cited in AI answers, not just which ones rank, and rewrite the uncited ones as direct, quotable answers.
  • If you are a builder: design for the answer layer, structured facts, fresh data, clear sourcing, because that is what both ChatGPT and Google's AI Overviews lift.
  • If you are just a user: notice when you stopped clicking through, and ask where that answer came from before you trust it, because an answer with no source is a rumour with good formatting.

The real pattern

The fight was never really ChatGPT versus Google. It is the question versus the link. For twenty years, searching meant getting a list and choosing. Now it increasingly means getting an answer and moving on. Whoever owns the answer owns the next decade, and the link, the humble thing that built the open web, is quietly becoming optional.

Final thoughts

Google is not dead, and ChatGPT has not won. Both can be true at once.

But something did flip this week. A chatbot company can now say "one fifth of Google" out loud, and the room does not laugh. It takes notes.

That, more than the number itself, is the story.
