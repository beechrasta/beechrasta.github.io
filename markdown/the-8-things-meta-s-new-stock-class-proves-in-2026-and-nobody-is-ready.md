# The 8 Things Meta's New Stock Class Proves in 2026, and Nobody Is Ready

*Everyone cheered the two-for-one split. Nobody asked what Meta just gave up in return. It gave up nothing. That is the point.*

Let's stop pretending a stock split is good news for shareholders.

Here's the uncomfortable truth: Meta just printed itself a new currency, kept all the votes, and told the market it's because AI costs too much money.

This article is not about whether Meta's AI bet pays off. It is about what the financing move reveals, and who gets to steer the ship while everyone else just rides.

## 1. It is not a stock split, it is a funding weapon

On October 21, alongside its Q3 earnings, **Meta** announced a new class of **non-voting Class C common stock**. Every existing shareholder gets one new share for each share they hold.

- It will have the economic effect of a **two-for-one split**
- The new shares start trading in **January 2027**
- They carry **zero votes**

The *so what*: call it a split if you want. A split does not let a company raise fresh capital, pay employees, or buy startups. This does.

*(Source: [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/business-and-practice/meta-plans-to-issue-new-non-voting-stock-semafor-reports))*

## 2. The earnings were great, which makes this weirder

**Meta** reported third-quarter revenue of **$64.6 billion**, up 26% from a year earlier and ahead of the $63.3 billion Wall Street expected. Adjusted earnings per share came in at **$3.83** against a $3.57 estimate.

- Q4 revenue guidance of **$73-76 billion**, above consensus
- The stock rose more than **4%** in after-hours trading

The *so what*: this is not a distressed company doing desperate things. It is a thriving company choosing to dilute everyone who does not vote.

*(Source: [MarketFlux](https://news.marketflux.io/news/meta-plans-nonvoting-shares-to-fund-ai-push-without-diluting-efdc27e75cfcc37d25c37e451af0e244.html))*

## 3. The $9.89 billion legal charge nobody put in the headline

Buried under the celebration: a **$9.89 billion legal charge** tied to a settlement with U.S. state attorneys general. It crushed operating income to **$12.99 billion**, far below the $20.85 billion analysts expected.

- One legal bill ate nearly half the quarter's operating profit
- Almost nobody in the coverage led with it

The *so what*: Meta is so profitable it can absorb a ten-billion-dollar legal settlement and still be up 4% after hours. Read that twice.

*(Source: [MarketFlux](https://news.marketflux.io/news/meta-plans-nonvoting-shares-to-fund-ai-push-without-diluting-efdc27e75cfcc37d25c37e451af0e244.html))*

## 4. Free cash flow just went negative, and that is the real story

**Meta** posted a free cash flow deficit of **$2.12 billion** in the quarter, against positive **$10.63 billion** a year earlier. Quarterly capital expenditure hit **$34.91 billion**.

- For years, Meta's defining trait was a cash geyser
- The geyser is now running in reverse

The *so what*: when the most cash-rich company in social media goes cash-negative in a quarter, the AI buildout is officially bigger than the business model that funds it.

*(Source: [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/164870-meta-lifts-2026-ai-spending-to-145b-adds-nonvoting-stock-class/))*

## 5. Full-year AI spending: $145 billion, and climbing

Meta raised the lower end of its 2026 capital expenditure forecast. It now expects capex of **$135-145 billion**, up from $130-145 billion, and full-year expenses of **$175-179 billion**.

- The money goes to data centers, **AI chips**, poached talent, and model training
- CFO **Susan Li** said the new share class expands Meta's "funding tools"

The *so what*: Meta is spending more on infrastructure in one year than most countries spend on their militaries. Of course it needs new funding tools.

*(Source: [The Brief](https://www.thebrief.news/en/standard/article/35058/meta-plans-non-voting-class-c-stock-to-fund-ai-spending))*

## 6. Zuckerberg loses zero control, and that is by design

Here is the cap table math. As of January 2026, Meta had about **2.19 billion Class A shares** and **342 million Class B shares** outstanding, and the Class B shares hold a **majority of the combined voting power**.

- Class C shares give investors economic exposure with **no say in how Meta is run**
- Meta says it will use them for acquisitions, **employee compensation**, and chip purchases
- One analyst warned the structure insulates management from scrutiny

The *so what*: <u>Meta can now raise billions, buy companies, and pay employees, all without Zuckerberg giving up a single vote.</u> That is not a split. That is a printing press with a lock on it.

*(Source: [The Brief](https://www.thebrief.news/en/standard/article/35058/meta-plans-non-voting-class-c-stock-to-fund-ai-spending))*

## 7. This is what the money is actually buying: Muse

**Zuckerberg** told analysts more than **10 million people** in the US and Canada set up **Muse** accounts in six weeks, and he expects it to eventually serve "billions of people" generating more revenue per user than the average Meta user.

- Meta is not charging upfront: it wants the network first
- "We think that it's going to be more valuable for people at scale when there are a lot of people and businesses using Muse," he said

The *so what*: the stock move is not funding a product. It is funding a land grab for the agent era, and Meta is paying for the land with paper it prints itself.

*(Source: [Reuters](https://wncy.com/2026/10/21/meta-to-issue-new-class-of-shares-as-it-tightens-ai-spending-forecast/))*

## 8. Meta has done this before, just not with stock

In 2025, Meta formed a joint venture with funds managed by **Blue Owl Capital** to develop the **Hyperion data center**. Blue Owl's funds own **80%**, Meta keeps 20%.

- The pattern: stretch the balance sheet with other people's money
- The new Class C shares are the equity version of the same playbook

The *so what*: Meta is not betting the company on AI. It is betting other people's money on AI, and keeping the steering wheel.

*(Source: [The Brief](https://www.thebrief.news/en/standard/article/35058/meta-plans-non-voting-class-c-stock-to-fund-ai-spending))*

## How to start

- **If you work at Meta:** your future RSUs may arrive in a share class that cannot vote. Price that in.
- **If you are a founder:** study this move. The lesson is not "print shares." It is "structure control before you need the money."

Meta's quarter proves there are now two AI economies. In one, giants print non-voting shares and spend $145 billion a year. In the other, everyone else is cutting a third of their AI projects because the bills got too big. Guess which economy you are in.

Wall Street cheered. The stock went up. And somewhere in Menlo Park, a new class of shares was born that lets one man spend $145 billion of other people's money without ever being outvoted.
