# The 9 Things Britain's AI Complaint Flood Proves in 2026, and Why Chatbots Just Broke the Complaint Box

Half the emails in one MP's office are AI-written now. Complaints went from two pages to two hundred. The people answering them are drowning, and nobody is paying for the extra work.

![A flat lay of many overlapping white and cream envelopes and gift tags](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/beechrasta/medium-images/main/2026-11-09/envelopes-hero.jpg)

**A pile of envelopes. In Britain, the letters inside them are increasingly written by chatbots, and the people who have to answer them are drowning. Photo by Joanna Kosinska on Unsplash.**

Let's stop pretending AI made complaining easier. It made complaining effortless, which is a very different thing, and the difference is currently burying Britain's public services.

In one Labour MP's office, about half the emails arriving every day are now written, in whole or in part, by AI. The caseworkers call it an onslaught. They started noticing it as a trickle in 2024.

This article is not about chatbots being bad. It is about what happens when writing becomes free and reading still costs the same.

## 1. Half the inbox is AI now

- **Hollie Wickens**, a senior caseworker in a Labour MP's office with a decade of experience, says about half the emails her office receives are some form of AI.
- **"It started as a trickle in 2024 and now it's an onslaught."**
- The AI-written mail makes requests and complaints much more complex and time-consuming to read and deal with, even for someone who has done this job for ten years.
- **ChatGPT** turns four later this month, alongside **Claude**, **Copilot**, and **Gemini**. The free legal adviser in everyone's pocket is four years old, and it just learned to write letters.

*(Source: [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/08/ai-clog-public-bodies-local-authorities-onslaught-chatbot-generated-correspondence))*

## 2. The two-page letter became a 200-page legal brief

- Before chatbots, the average complaint to a local authority ran to about **two pages**. **Deborah Evans**, chief executive of **Lawyers in Local Government**, which represents 5,600 council lawyers, says the majority are now **10 times longer**.
- The worst case she knows of ran to about **200 pages**. Two hundred pages, for one complaint.
- AI can make a complaint sound professional and formal. It can also treat a complaint as a legal claim, citing statute and case law that may come from another jurisdiction, such as Canada, and it can hallucinate.
- Councils spot the machine-written ones by their length, certain phrases, and heavy use of bullet points. And here is the kicker: **local authorities have one pot of money and get no extra money because of more complaints.**

*(Source: [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/08/ai-clog-public-bodies-local-authorities-onslaught-chatbot-generated-correspondence))*

## 3. The parking ticket that became a legal case

- **Alessandro Georgiou**, leader of Enfield council in London, reports a significant uptick in residents using AI to appeal council decisions, including parking fines.
- The flood covers everything: adult social care services, missed bin collections, objections to planning applications. Nothing is too small to become a twenty-page legal argument anymore.
- Evans put the trade-off bluntly. Every pound spent dealing with complaints is a pound not spent on something else.
- <u>Your parking appeal is now competing for the same money as someone's social care.</u> That is what "free" writing actually costs.

*(Source: [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/08/ai-clog-public-bodies-local-authorities-onslaught-chatbot-generated-correspondence))*

## 4. The tribunal surge nobody planned for

- Employment tribunal claims opened between April and June rose **28%** compared with a year earlier, representing **14,000 cases** from single claimants, according to the most recent government figures.
- Concluded cases fell over the same period, leaving **70,000 open single claims** at the end of June. The backlog is growing faster than the system can clear it.
- A technology start-up founder told the **Guardian** that a former employee dismissed on performance grounds is pursuing a tribunal claim, and the founder expects to have spent **£30,000** on legal fees by the hearing.
- **Jim Moore**, an employee relations expert at **Hamilton Nash**, says many of these complaints are unnecessarily verbose, with embellished or exaggerated claims, and often fail to state what resolution the complainant actually wants.

*(Source: [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/08/ai-clog-public-bodies-local-authorities-onslaught-chatbot-generated-correspondence))*

## 5. Transport for London's record FOI pile

- **Transport for London** received a record **4,769 freedom of information requests** in 2025-26, a 5% increase on its previous peak in 2023-24.
- Minutes of its audit and assurance committee reported a noted increase in appeals that appeared AI-generated, often containing highly technical and often inaccurate assertions and assumptions.
- FOI requests are a legal right, and that is exactly the problem. You cannot refuse a badly written one. You have to answer it, carefully, by a human, on a deadline.
- The system was designed for citizens asking questions. It was not designed for a machine asking a thousand questions at once.

*(Source: [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/uk-public-bodies-hit-by-surge-of-ai-generated-complaints))*

## 6. The watchdog's warning: the wrong answers still need human answers

- The **Information Commissioner's Office**, the UK body that oversees information rights, found that many public authorities received more requests in 2025.
- The AI-looking ones tend to be very long, repetitive, contain unrelated comments or misdirected demands, have greater scope, and make it harder to identify the information actually requested.
- The ICO cautions that AI-drafted requests containing inaccuracies still need to be answered by humans. A hallucinated request does not get a hallucinated answer. A person has to untangle it first.
- This is the part nobody talks about. **AI does not just generate the letter. It generates the work of disproving the letter.**

*(Source: [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/08/ai-clog-public-bodies-local-authorities-onslaught-chatbot-generated-correspondence))*

## 7. The humans are drowning

- **Vonnie Sandlan**, head of office for **Graeme Downie**, Labour MP for Dunfermline and Dollar, says staff are exhausted, stretched to the limits, and working more hours than they are paid.
- Her office has stopped responding to automated "click to email your MP" campaigns, just to focus on real constituents. The spam filter is now a human being giving up.
- **Lisa Gillmore**, president of the **GMB** union's MPs' and peers' staff branch, says casework has doubled or more in offices that keep their own figures.
- MPs, local authorities, lawyers, schools, courts, and businesses are all reporting the same thing. **Every sector is experiencing very similar issues**, says Evans. Nobody was ready, and nobody got extra staff.

*(Source: [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/08/ai-clog-public-bodies-local-authorities-onslaught-chatbot-generated-correspondence))*

## 8. The name for it: agentic flooding

- Researchers at the **Centre for Digital Governance** at the **Hertie School** in Berlin, the **Cooperative AI Foundation**, and **GovAI** have a technical name for this: **"agentic flooding."** Commenters call it **"AI clog."**
- Their suggested fixes are blunt: make applications harder, charge fees, or block bots. In other words, put the friction back that AI removed.
- Some ombudsman services have already started imposing word limits or using online templates. The complaint box is getting a bouncer.
- AI was even credited with some of this year's better-than-expected UK GDP growth. The same tool boosting the economy is clogging the institutions that run it.

*(Source: [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/08/ai-clog-public-bodies-local-authorities-onslaught-chatbot-generated-correspondence))*

## 9. The fix is more AI, obviously

- **Wiltshire council** is exploring AI to summarise long emails and draft initial responses. **Stroud district council** discussed using **Microsoft Copilot** for reports.
- The UK government has advised civil servants to verify AI-provided facts, not input personal data, and ensure humans review work and take final decisions.
- Technology journalist **Jamie Bartlett** says public bodies and companies will probably have to use AI filtering or stricter acceptance rules, or risk being completely overwhelmed.
- And if you think this is a British problem, wait. India's own grievance portals run on the same logic, and the same chatbots are free here too. The pattern travels faster than the policy.

*(Source: [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/08/ai-clog-public-bodies-local-authorities-onslaught-chatbot-generated-correspondence))*

## How to start

- **If you are a citizen with a real complaint:** write it short, say what happened, and say what you want fixed. A two-page letter that names the resolution beats a 200-page legal brief that forgets to.
- **If you run a public body or a support inbox:** templates, word limits, and a triage layer are no longer nice to have. They are the job now.
- **If you build AI tools:** your product is now the other side's workload. Design for the reader, not just the writer.
- **If you are a developer:** the next big govtech product is not a chatbot that writes complaints. It is the filter that reads them.

## The real pattern

Writing used to be the bottleneck that kept complaints honest, short, and proportionate. AI removed the bottleneck from one side only. The cost did not disappear, it moved: from the person with the grievance to the person paid to answer it. Every system that makes expression free without making comprehension cheaper is a tax on whoever is stuck reading.

## Final thoughts

The complaint box was never designed for infinite letters.

Sooner or later, somebody has to read them.
