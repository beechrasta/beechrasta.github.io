# The 8 Things Anthropic's IPO Filing Proves in 2026, and Why Public Investors Get the Money but Not the Wheel

*Anthropic just filed to go public while warning, in its own paperwork, that its AI could pose catastrophic risks. You can buy the stock. You just cannot steer the company.*

![Anthropic IPO filing artwork](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/beechrasta/medium-images/main/2026-10-28/anthropic-ipo-hero.jpg)

*Anthropic's IPO filing, the document that turns a private AI lab into a public company. Photo: Ars Technica*

Let's stop pretending an IPO filing is a victory lap. **Anthropic's** S-1, made public on October 26, 2026, is a sales pitch and a warning label stapled together.

Here is the uncomfortable truth: **the most safety obsessed AI lab on Earth is asking public markets for money while keeping public investors away from power.**

This article is not a stock tip. It is about what happens when the company building **Claude** goes public, and what that deal means for developers, users, and everyone downstream of its models.

## 1. The revenue is real, and so is the loss

- **Anthropic** reported **$16.3 billion** in revenue for the first half of 2026, up from **$1.16 billion** a year earlier.
- Its net loss for the same period was about **$80.7 billion**.
- Most of that loss is not cash burned on servers. It is a paper revaluation of financing instruments, the kind of accounting charge that explodes when a private valuation explodes.

The operating picture is far calmer, and far more useful. The filing shows an operating loss of roughly **$693 million** on that **$16.3 billion** of revenue.

**So the headline loss is scary, but the business underneath it is not on fire.**

*(Source: [XTB](https://www.xtb.com/en/market-analysis/anthropic-files-prospectus-ahead-of-ipo-with-ant-ticker-on-nasdaq-the-launch-date-and-price-remain-a-mystery))*

## 2. The compute bill is the real business model

- **Anthropic** spent nearly **$9 billion** on AI infrastructure in the first half of 2026, up from **$2.3 billion** a year earlier.
- Its future minimum payment commitments run into the hundreds of billions, tied to cloud and hardware deals with **Google**, **Amazon**, **Microsoft**, and **Broadcom**.
- One tally of its multiyear commitments puts the total above **$500 billion**, including roughly **$161 billion** in **Broadcom** leases alone.

Read that twice. **Anthropic** sells a service customers can scale down, and pays for capacity it cannot.

**That mismatch, flexible revenue against fixed costs, is the central risk of the whole IPO.**

*(Source: [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/oct/26/anthropic-stock-market-filing-takeaways))*

## 3. The valuation is a rumor the filing refuses to confirm

- Some investors have valued **Anthropic** at **$1.5 trillion to $2 trillion**.
- The S-1 itself lists no target valuation.
- Reports point to a possible mid November debut, with talk of a raise around **$100 billion**, which would be the largest in history by a wide margin.

*In other words, the market is pricing a legend while the paperwork prices nothing.*

**If you buy on the rumor, you are buying a story the company itself declined to put in writing.**

*(Source: [Particle](https://particle.news/story/anthropic-files-s1-for-nasdaq-listing-lays-out-soaring-revenue-giant-accounting-losses-and-safety))*

## 4. The founders keep 50.1 percent of the votes

- **Anthropic's** seven founders jointly hold a special class of stock giving a majority of them **50.1 percent** of shareholder voting power.
- That lets them elect three members of the board.
- If the founders ever split with no majority, only then do their shares vote in proportion with common stock.

**Public shareholders get an economic interest. They do not get control.**

*(Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/27/whos-really-in-charge-at-anthropic-after-the-ipo/))*

## 5. A trust you did not elect picks most of the board

- **Anthropic** is a public benefit corporation, legally allowed to weigh its mission alongside profit.
- Its **Long-Term Benefit Trust** owns a class of stock that lets it elect and remove a majority of the board, known as **Class T** directors.
- The trust is chaired by **Neil Shah**, formerly of **GiveWell**, and includes former **Federal Reserve** chair **Ben Bernanke**, national security expert **Richard Fontaine**, and **Harvard** physicist **Christopher Stubbs**.

![Anthropic's governance structure](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/beechrasta/medium-images/main/2026-10-28/anthropic-governance.png)

*The governance map: founders, trust, board, and common shareholders, in that order. Photo: TechCrunch*

There is one check on the trust. When it nominates or removes a specified director without cause, the board's nominating committee can refuse and throw the decision to employees for a vote.

**Anthropic formalized what OpenAI learned the hard way: in an AI lab, employees are a constituency, not just staff.**

*(Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/27/whos-really-in-charge-at-anthropic-after-the-ipo/))*

## 6. The filing warns about its own product in plain language

- **Anthropic** warns its models could, without safeguards, **pose catastrophic or existential risks to humanity**, especially if misused, replicated, weaponized, or run with high autonomy.
- It says its safeguards may be incomplete, outdated, circumvented, or fail.
- It groups AI risks as misuse by bad actors, loss of control, authoritarian abuse, and economic disruption.

This is not a critic's blog post. This is the company's own risk section, written for investors, reviewed by lawyers.

<u>When a company warns you about itself this bluntly, believe the warning more than the marketing.</u>

*(Source: [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/10/anthropic-ipo-warns-its-ai-could-blackmail-people-become-sentient-end-humanity/))*

## 7. The cyberattack claim is the strangest line in the filing

- **Anthropic** says it detected what it believes is the first documented large scale cyberattack with little human intervention.
- A **Chinese state sponsored** hacking group allegedly manipulated a **Claude** code tool in attempted infiltration of roughly 30 targets, including tech companies, financial institutions, chemical manufacturers, and government agencies.
- The company also warns AI could help authoritarian governments deepen repression and build high tech surveillance states.

The filing also notes a standoff with the **Trump** administration after **Anthropic** refused to loosen restrictions barring use for domestic surveillance or autonomous lethal weapons.

**A company going public is telling you its tool was allegedly turned into a hacking assistant, and its response was to hold the line anyway.**

*(Source: [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/oct/26/anthropic-stock-market-filing-takeaways))*

## 8. The customer base is enterprise, and concentrated

- **Anthropic's** top five customers account for **25 percent** of its business.
- About 250 customers pay more than **$10 million** a year, and over 1,900 pay more than **$1 million**.
- Unlike **OpenAI**, **Anthropic** emphasizes business customers over consumers.

That concentration cuts both ways. Big enterprise deals are sticky and lucrative, and losing one of them hurts in a way losing a thousand chat users never would.

**If you build on Claude, you are building on a vendor whose biggest customers matter more than you do. Price and policy accordingly.**

*(Source: [XTB](https://www.xtb.com/en/market-analysis/anthropic-files-prospectus-ahead-of-ipo-with-ant-ticker-on-nasdaq-the-launch-date-and-price-remain-a-mystery))*

## How to start if you build on Claude

- **For developers:** pin your model version, and keep a second provider warm. A vendor this concentrated can change terms fast.
- **For startups:** treat **Claude's** safety posture as a feature, not a guarantee. Your users will blame you, not the model, when something goes wrong.
- **For investors:** read the risk section before the revenue chart. The filing is unusually honest, and honesty is data.
- **For everyone else:** remember who actually votes here. Founders first, trust second, employees as tiebreaker, public shareholders last.

## The real pattern

Anthropic is trying to solve the oldest problem in tech, how to take public money without taking public orders, by writing the answer into its corporate DNA before anyone can vote on it. The IPO is not the company selling out its mission. It is the company building a legal fortress around it, then inviting you to fund the fortress from outside the walls.

## Final thoughts

I like that this filing exists. A frontier lab telling the public, in a legal document, that its own product could be dangerous is more honest than a thousand safety blog posts.

But honesty is not control. If you buy **ANT**, buy it knowing exactly what you are buying: a share of the profits, and almost none of the power.
