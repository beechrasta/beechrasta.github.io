# The 7 Things Big Tech's 122 Billion Dollar Fundraising Week Proves in 2026, and Why the Chipmaker Just Bought Its Customer

*Microsoft borrowed 25 billion dollars it did not need. Meta raised 97.5 billion it could not fund from cash flow. And Nvidia, the company selling both of them the chips, bought a piece of the customer.*

![Meta's headquarters in Menlo Park, California](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/beechrasta/medium-images/main/2026-10-30/meta-hq.jpg)

*Meta's Menlo Park headquarters. The company just raised the largest equity sum in its history to pay for AI data centers. Photo: Law.com*

Let's stop pretending the AI boom is paid for out of profits anymore. This week, the richest companies on earth went to the bond market and the stock market with their hands out, in the same week, for the same reason.

Here is the uncomfortable truth: **the AI buildout is now financed, not earned, and the company selling the shovels just bought a share of the mine.**

This article is not a crash prediction. It is about what changed this week, who is lending the money, and why the circle of cash matters more than any single number.

## 1. Microsoft borrowed 25 billion dollars for the first time in nine years

**Microsoft** is planning to raise about 25 billion dollars through its first US investment grade bond sale since 2017, when it raised 17 billion. The deal is split across as many as 10 tranches, with maturities from 2 years all the way to 40 years, into 2066.

- Orders topped 100 billion dollars, more than four times the offering, so investors are not exactly running away.
- **Wells Fargo**, **JPMorgan Chase**, and **UBS** are managing the sale.
- **Microsoft** pointed **Reuters** to a regulatory filing confirming the sale without disclosing the size, and its shares slipped about 1 percent on the news.

One day earlier, finance chief **Amy Hood** told investors on the earnings call that the company would pursue additional funding "when we believe it is attractive to do so," to preserve cash flow and financial flexibility. Translation: the cash machine still runs, but the data center bill now runs faster.

*(Source: [Reuters via SRN News](https://srnnews.com/microsoft-expects-to-raise-25-billion-in-us-bond-sale-source-says/))*

## 2. Microsoft is the strong one, which is exactly why this matters

Here is the detail that should worry you more than the headline. **Microsoft** is one of only two US listed companies holding top tier AAA ratings from both **Moody's** and **S&P Global**, alongside **Johnson and Johnson**.

- It generated 58 billion dollars in operating cash in the September 2026 quarter, and 18.6 billion after property and equipment spending.
- It expects to keep generating positive free cash flow through June 2027, a standout among hyperscalers that have started burning cash.
- Even so, its capital spending plus finance leases hit 45.2 billion dollars in the latest quarter, up about 30 percent year over year.

<u>If the strongest balance sheet in tech is borrowing, borrowing is no longer a sign of weakness. It is the business model.</u> And the 40 year tranche, due 2066, will test how much extra yield bond buyers demand to finance AI buildouts even from a AAA issuer.

*(Source: [The Business Times](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/international/microsoft-looks-raise-least-us25-billion-us-bond-sale?ref=article-bottom-more-in-companies-markets_energy-commodities))*

## 3. Meta raised 97.5 billion dollars, the biggest equity sum in its history

**Meta** raised its planned equity fundraising to 97.5 billion dollars on October 27, the largest in the company's history, to pay for AI infrastructure. The plan breaks into three pieces.

- 25 billion dollars of common stock at 625 dollars per share, upsized from 22.5 billion.
- 250 million convertible preferred depositary shares at 50 dollars each, about 12.5 billion dollars.
- Up to 60 billion more through an at the market program starting in early 2027, selling shares gradually at prevailing prices.

The context is brutal. **Meta** reported a 2.12 billion dollar cash burn in the third quarter, its first negative free cash flow in over a decade, against positive 10.63 billion a year earlier. It has committed to about 825 billion dollars in future spending, mostly data centers and AI hardware, and expects 2026 capital expenditure of 135 to 145 billion dollars.

*(Source: [Reuters via Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/world-news/meta-eyes-record-97-5-billion-equity-raise-to-fund-ai-infrastructure-push-126102700558_1.html))*

## 4. The chipmakers bought the customer

This is the part of the story that reads like satire until you check the filing. **Meta's** prospectus named **Nvidia**, **AMD**, and **Broadcom** as interested in buying up to 5 billion dollars of the shares combined. Then two of them named their stakes.

- **Nvidia** committed 3 billion dollars to **Meta's** equity offering, alongside a deeper partnership on **Muse**, **Meta's** personal AI agent.
- **Meta** will expand deployment of **Nvidia's Vera Rubin** systems and **Vera CPUs**, plus **Spectrum-X** networking and **BlueField-4** chips.
- **AMD** followed with 1 billion dollars. Its **Helios** rack systems are already installed and being tested at **Meta**, and its **EPYC** processors run the computers behind **Muse**.

![Nvidia's headquarters, now belonging to a company that owns a piece of its biggest customer](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/beechrasta/medium-images/main/2026-10-30/nvidia-building.jpg)

*Nvidia's headquarters in Santa Clara. The chipmaker is now a shareholder in one of its largest buyers. Photo: Ceotech*

So the supplier of the most expensive hardware in the deal is also a shareholder in the buyer, with a direct financial interest in the buyer buying more of its hardware. **Nvidia** is not betting on **Meta's** ads business. It is betting that always on AI agents are the dominant compute workload of the 2030s, and that **Vera Rubin** captures that spend.

*(Source: [Nvidia announcement via TechTimes](https://Www.techtimes.com/articles/329589/20261027/nvidia-takes-3b-meta-equity-stake-turning-biggest-customer-strategic-partner.htm))*

## 5. The circle is much bigger than one deal

Zoom out, and the **Meta** deal is one loop in a web of them. **Nvidia** has invested in **OpenAI**, **Anthropic**, **Intel**, and **xAI**, and data firm **Dealroom** counts 121 investment rounds from **Nvidia** so far in 2026, against 13 in 2022.

- **Nvidia** invests in **CoreWeave**, which holds a 6.3 billion dollar capacity purchase agreement with **Nvidia**.
- **SpaceX** agreed a roughly 40 billion dollar GPU leasing arrangement, with an **Apollo** affiliate buying **Nvidia Vera Rubin** chips and leasing them back.
- **Alphabet** priced 25 billion dollars of debt on August 6, the first blue chip hyperscaler bond sale before **Microsoft's**.

Analysts at **William Blair** said they "expect investors to continue to question the financing and potential for future capital raises." That is analyst speak for: nobody can see the whole circle from outside, and the disclosure rules were not written for suppliers owning customers.

*(Source: [QNC News](https://qncnews.com/2026/10/27/meta-lifts-record-share-sale-to-97-5-billion-to-fund-ai-push/))*

## 6. Wall Street noticed, and it blinked

The market reaction this week was not a panic, but it was a tell. **Meta's** shares fell on the fundraising news, **Microsoft's** slipped about 1 percent, and a rotation out of AI winners hit the Nasdaq on spending worries.

- **Alphabet's** capital spending of 51.36 billion dollars in the quarter fell short of analyst expectations of 55.09 billion, and investors punished it anyway.
- Shares of neocloud providers like **CoreWeave** and **Nebius** weakened in after hours trading when **Microsoft's** spending came in below estimates.
- The pattern is now familiar: spend more than expected and get punished for the bill, spend less than expected and get punished for the slowdown.

## 7. Why this hits you even if you never buy a stock

You do not need a brokerage account for this to reach you. You need a cloud bill, a job in tech, or a graphics card.

- Data center spending sets the price of compute, and compute prices set the price of every AI product you use, including the free ones.
- When the bill is financed with 40 year bonds and at the market share sales, the pressure to monetize harder, faster, and more aggressively only grows.
- When suppliers own customers, the incentive to keep the buildout going survives any single quarter's earnings, because stopping would hurt both sides of the same trade.

The bill does not vanish because the financing is clever. It just arrives later, wearing a different name.

## How to start

- **If you are a developer:** watch your cloud provider's capital spending against its cash flow, not its keynote slides. That ratio decides your prices next year.
- **If you work in tech:** ask where your employer's AI budget sits in this circle. A customer funded by its supplier is a different risk than a customer funded by its profits.
- **If you just use this stuff:** expect the free tier to keep shrinking. Somebody has to service 122 billion dollars of new financing, and it will not be the shareholders alone.

## The real pattern

Phase one of the AI boom was paid for with profits. Phase two is paid for with debt, equity, and the suppliers' own money, lent back to the buyers. The technology did not get cheaper. The financing got more creative, and creativity in financing is what you do right before the bill comes due, not after.

## Final thoughts

None of this means AI is a bubble about to pop on Monday. It means the boom now has creditors, and creditors are patient until they are not. The most interesting question in tech is no longer what the models can do. It is who is paying for the buildings they run in, and what happens when the lenders ask for their money back.
