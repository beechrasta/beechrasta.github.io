# The 8 Things Sam Altman's RAM Rant Proves in 2026, and Why Your Next PC Costs a Cloud Tax

Your next laptop is more expensive because a data center ate its memory. The CEO of the company eating it just said that is how it should be.

![Sam Altman](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/beechrasta/medium-images/main/2026-11-01/altman-hero.jpg)
*Sam Altman, OpenAI CEO, on the Cheeky Pint podcast, where he called personal DRAM a poor use of a scarce resource. Credit: Getty Images via Moneywise.*

Let's stop pretending the AI boom is free for everyone else.

On October 29, **OpenAI** CEO **Sam Altman** went on **Stripe** co-founder **John Collison's** Cheeky Pint podcast and said something PC builders will not forgive soon: from a global allocation perspective, it is "quite terrible" for everybody to have a bunch of the world's limited supply of **DRAM** sitting in their own computer, used 5 percent of the time.

This article is not about one tone deaf quote. It is about the memory shortage behind it, the prices it is already setting, and why your hardware budget is now competing with the biggest companies on Earth.

## 1. What Altman actually said, and the question he was answering

Altman was not ranting about your gaming rig unprompted. Collison asked about local AI, the idea that open source models running on personal computers could ease the need for giant data centers.

Altman pushed back hard. Memory in a data center serves thousands of users around the clock, he argued, while the RAM in your laptop sits idle most of the day. "You want maximum utilisation in the cloud if you're trying to deal with this very supply-constrained environment," he said, "and somehow many people's intuition is the opposite of that."

He also admitted the market is broken from his side too: "I think the invisible hand is failing right now."

*(Source: [Moneywise, Sam Altman says the RAM in your PC is a waste](https://moneywise.com/news/top-stories/sam-altman-dram-pc-openai))*

## 2. He is technically right, and that is exactly why people are furious

Here is the uncomfortable bit: on pure utilisation, Altman is not wrong. A server rack runs near full tilt. Your laptop does not.

But that framing skips why people run local AI at all. Privacy, no monthly subscription, no account, no one else's terms of service. Efficiency is not the only value a computer has, and treating it as one is a very data center way of seeing the world.

It also skips who created the scarcity. **OpenAI** is one of the biggest buyers of the very memory Altman calls scarce, and critics were quick to point out that AI companies are driving the shortage they now want consumers to accommodate.

*(Source: [Moneywise, Sam Altman says the RAM in your PC is a waste](https://moneywise.com/news/top-stories/sam-altman-dram-pc-openai))*

## 3. The price tag is already brutal

This is not a theoretical debate. Conventional **DRAM** contract prices jumped roughly **93 to 98 percent** in the first quarter of 2026 alone, according to **TrendForce**. The firm projected a further 10 to 15 percent rise in the fourth quarter, and warned PC memory could shrink next year as more production shifts to servers.

At checkout, a 32GB kit of **DDR5** now runs about **500 dollars**, up from roughly 400 dollars in early April, in a **Tom's Guide** investigation that calls the markup a "cloud tax."

The cheapest 32GB **DDR5**-6000 kit on the market climbed to **449 dollars**, while a **Corsair** 32GB **DDR5**-4800 kit that once sold for 80 dollars now costs **399 dollars**.

*(Source: [Moneywise, Sam Altman says the RAM in your PC is a waste](https://moneywise.com/news/top-stories/sam-altman-dram-pc-openai))*

## 4. Japan's 761 percent warning sign

If you want one number that shows how wild this market has become: **DDR5** prices in Japan have jumped as much as **761 percent** in a year, with one 128GB kit now costing **3,300 dollars**.

That is not a typo and not a luxury product. That is memory, the most boring commodity in computing, behaving like a speculative asset.

When a commodity behaves like that, it means the people buying it are not price sensitive. Data center operators with trillion dollar buildouts are not haggling over RAM kits. You are competing with buyers who do not feel the price.

*(Source: [TweakTown, Altman says your PC's RAM is a poor use of DRAM](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/114419/sam-altman-says-your-pcs-ram-is-a-quite-terrible-use-of-the-worlds-limited-dram-supply/index.html))*

## 5. OpenAI's own appetite is staggering

In October 2025, Altman signed letters of intent with **Samsung** and **SK Hynix** to supply memory for **Stargate**, **OpenAI's** data center buildout. **SK Group** said the projected demand could reach up to **900,000 DRAM wafers a month**.

Based on **TechInsights'** 2025 production estimate, that is as much as **40 percent** of global output.

Treat that as a reported figure, not a confirmed one, since **OpenAI** has not broken the numbers down publicly. But even half of it would make **OpenAI** the single biggest force in the memory market, which is worth remembering the next time someone tells you consumers are the ones misallocating supply.

*(Source: [Moneywise, Sam Altman says the RAM in your PC is a waste](https://moneywise.com/news/top-stories/sam-altman-dram-pc-openai))*

## 6. The memory makers are not riding to your rescue

**SK Hynix** just reported a record quarterly operating profit of **83.3 trillion won**, about **62 billion dollars**, said more than half its production for 2027 and 2028 is already booked under long term deals, and warned the shortage could last through **2030**.

**Micron** went further. In December 2025 it announced it would exit its **Crucial** consumer business after 29 years, ending retail sales of **Crucial** RAM and SSDs by February 2026, to redirect capacity to AI data center customers. Its chief business officer **Sumit Sadana** said plainly that AI driven data center growth created a surge in demand, and the company chose its larger strategic customers.

When a beloved consumer brand dies so its factory can serve data centers, the shortage is not a blip. It is a restructuring.

*(Source: [Times of India, memory price hike](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/technology/tech-news/phones-laptops-and-consoles-get-costlier-over-memory-price-hike-openai-ceo-sam-altman-to-gadget-buyers-having-dram-in-your-devices-is-quite-terrible-for-the-worlds-limited-supply/articleshow/135359311.cms))*

## 7. It is hitting India and laptops, not just gaming rigs

This is not only a PC builder problem. **OnePlus**, **Samsung**, and **Oppo** have revised phone prices more than once this year because of rising memory costs, and industry watchers say mobile grade memory now costs several times what it did a year ago, putting phones under **Rs 10,000** at risk.

On the PC side, **HP** has said memory made up about **35 percent** of a laptop's production cost in July, up from 15 to 18 percent a quarter earlier. **Intel**, reporting on October 29, forecast fourth quarter revenue of 15 to 16 billion dollars against roughly 17 billion expected, saw its shares fall over 20 percent in extended trading, and expects the PC market to shrink **12 percent** this year and another **10 percent** in 2027.

<u>When memory is a third of a laptop's cost, every laptop gets worse or pricier. There is no third option.</u>

## 8. Even OpenAI is feeling the money squeeze

Here is the twist that makes Altman's timing so awkward. While he argued the world's memory belongs in data centers, **OpenAI** was struggling to fund those data centers.

**Bloomberg** reported on September 29 that the company sought at least **30 billion dollars** at a pre money valuation of about **1.4 trillion dollars**. By October 22 the price had slipped to **1.25 trillion dollars**, and by the October 30 investor deadline the round had been cut to about **20 billion dollars**, with the **Wall Street Journal** reporting **OpenAI** raised less than half its original target.

So the company telling you to give up your RAM is itself finding it harder to raise the money to buy that RAM. The AI buildout is not an unstoppable force with infinite funding. It is a very expensive bet, and the memory market is where ordinary buyers are paying part of the price.

*(Source: [Moneywise, Sam Altman says the RAM in your PC is a waste](https://moneywise.com/news/top-stories/sam-altman-dram-pc-openai))*

## How to start

- **If you are buying a PC now:** buy the memory you actually need today, not the upgrade you planned for next year. Waiting is not a guaranteed win while supply stays tight.
- **If you are a student or first time builder:** compare specs line by line instead of sticker prices. Brands are trimming storage in mainstream models to hold prices down.
- **If you run local AI models:** your setup is exactly what Altman dismissed, and it is also your privacy advantage. Budget for memory as the expensive part, not the afterthought.
- **If you are a developer:** treat workstation memory as infrastructure. A 32GB floor for serious local work is no longer generous, it is the entry ticket.

## The real pattern

Every AI era has a moment when the industry stops selling you a tool and starts billing you for its inputs. First it was your data, then your attention, then your subscription. Now it is your hardware budget. The memory in your PC became a contested resource the day data centers started buying it by the wafer, and no CEO quote will uncontest it. The question is not whether AI needs the memory. It is who pays when there is not enough to go around, and right now the answer is you.

## Final thoughts

Altman is right about utilisation and wrong about what a computer is for. A PC is not a badly run server. It is the one machine you actually own, that works without a subscription, that keeps your data in your room.

If the AI industry wants your RAM, it should build more of it, not talk you out of yours. Until then, buy what you need, keep your old machine alive a little longer, and remember that the "cloud tax" on your next upgrade is not a law of nature. It is a choice made by companies with bigger wallets than yours.
