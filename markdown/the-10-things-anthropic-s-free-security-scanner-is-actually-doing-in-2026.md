# The 10 Things Anthropic's Free Security Scanner Is Actually Doing in 2026

*Everyone is arguing about AI taking jobs. Anthropic just volunteered its best models to guard power grids and patch your open-source repo for free.*

Let's stop pretending AI safety is a research paper. On October 8, 2026, Anthropic turned it into a security company.

Here is the uncomfortable truth: their models already found more than 29,000 candidate vulnerabilities in six months. They had no choice but to deal with them.

This article is not a product launch recap. It is about what happens when the company that built the weapon starts giving away the shield.

### 1. There is a new umbrella, and it has a dramatic name

Anthropic grouped its security work under one banner: the **Anthropic Cyber Mission**.

- Announced **October 8, 2026**, it covers two programs: the free **OSS Scanner** for open source and a **critical infrastructure defense program**.
- Earlier the same week, Anthropic folded **Project Glasswing** (which gave vetted organizations access to **Claude Mythos** since April 2026) into an expanded **Cyber Verification Program**.
- The stated lesson from Glasswing: finding vulnerabilities has never been easier, but verifying, prioritizing, and fixing them remains hard. And it "has not yet cut cyber risk by enough."

The so what: **this is a strategy, not a stunt.** *(Source: [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/08/anthropic-launches-critical-infrastructure-program-and-free-oss-scanner-for-open-source/))*

### 2. Anthropic will scan your open-source project for free

The **OSS Scanner** is a free, opt-in, periodic vulnerability-scanning service for eligible open-source projects.

- It runs on Anthropic's **strongest models**, the frontier **Claude** family.
- It is funded by the **Defender Advantage Fund**, which Anthropic set up in August 2026.
- Anthropic also put money into the **Python Software Foundation**, the **Apache Software Foundation**, and **Alpha-Omega** and **OpenSSF** via the **Linux Foundation**.

The so what: **the best model Anthropic has, pointed at your code, for zero dollars.** *(Source: [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/anthropic-cybersecurity-program-critical-infrastructure-open-source/))*

### 3. Every report ships with a working exploit

This is the part that made me sit up. The scanner does not just flag problems.

- Each report includes a **self-contained reproducer**: a proof of concept showing the vulnerability actually works.
- Where the model can manage it, you also get a **candidate patch** plus a code-history **bisection** showing exactly when the flaw was introduced.
- Every finding comes with an explanation and suggested patching options, so the fix is essentially clear for an engineer.

The so what: **they do not just tell you there is a hole. They hand you the weapon and the bandage.** *(Source: [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/08/anthropic-launches-critical-infrastructure-program-and-free-oss-scanner-for-open-source/))*

### 4. The reports skip human review, mistakes included

Reports go **straight to maintainers with no human review**, because speed matters more than polish.

- Anthropic admits some reports will contain mistakes, like **wrong severity ratings**.
- In a pre-launch accuracy test, expert pen testers checked **97 critical and high-severity findings across 48 projects** and cleared 85 for disclosure.
- Of the remaining 12, all but one were real bugs that simply duplicated known issues or other scanners' findings.

The so what: **speed over perfection is the entire design philosophy.** *(Source: [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/08/anthropic-launches-critical-infrastructure-program-and-free-oss-scanner-for-open-source/))*

### 5. The accuracy numbers are surprisingly good

Early trial results suggest this is not a demo dressed up as a program.

- Embedded encryption library maker **wolfSSL** received 74 reports during early trials. All but two were valid.
- Five of those wolfSSL reports became **CVEs**, the official cataloged vulnerabilities the industry tracks.
- Remember the scale here: these are findings in widely used software, generated by a model, with patches attached.

The so what: **this is already producing real CVEs, not conference slides.** *(Source: [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/08/anthropic-launches-critical-infrastructure-program-and-free-oss-scanner-for-open-source/))*

### 6. This exists because Anthropic drowned in its own findings

The scanner was born from a backlog problem, which is the most honest origin story in tech.

- Over the past six months, Anthropic's models turned up **more than 29,000 candidate vulnerabilities** in widely used software.
- Human staff manually reviewed about **6,000** of them.
- Nearly **5,000 unreviewed reports**, complete with proposed patches, were already sent to maintainers who asked for the full batch.
- The setup is openly inspired by **Google's OSS-Fuzz**.

The so what: **the scanner is a backlog solution wearing a superhero cape.** *(Source: [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/08/anthropic-launches-critical-infrastructure-program-and-free-oss-scanner-for-open-source/))*

### 7. Enrollment is a GitHub pull request

Getting in is refreshingly un-corporate for a program this ambitious.

- Core maintainers enroll by submitting a **pull request to an Anthropic GitHub repository**.
- Eligibility follows the Google OSS-Fuzz test: **critical impact on infrastructure and user security**, decided case by case.
- Projects without the staff to handle raw findings still get **human-verified reports** through Anthropic's existing disclosure process.

The so what: **open source, for open source, through open source.** *(Source: [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/08/anthropic-launches-critical-infrastructure-program-and-free-oss-scanner-for-open-source/))*

### 8. Eleven companies now guard the power grid with Claude

The second program is the **critical infrastructure defense** effort, and the partner list reads like a security conference attendee sheet.

- Founding partners (11): **Accenture**, **Booz Allen Hamilton**, **Deloitte**, **PwC**, **Dragos**, **Insane Cyber**, **Nozomi Networks**, **CrowdStrike**, **Palo Alto Networks**, **Hitachi**, and **Rockwell Automation**.
- Anthropic brings **frontier Claude models**, onsite engineers, and threat research to the firms protecting power grids and water systems.
- Several partners already use Claude to **scan and patch code**, advise which fixes are safe to apply to running systems, and help with incident response and red-teaming.

The so what: **this is AI inside the substation, not AI in a slide deck.** *(Source: [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/anthropic-cybersecurity-program-critical-infrastructure-open-source/))*

### 9. More than half of U.S. states got the offer

This is not a pilot. The distribution is already nation-scale.

- Anthropic has offered **frontier models and technical support to more than half of U.S. states** and to large operators of critical infrastructure.
- The target is **operational technology**: plants, substations, and water systems where equipment must run for decades and often cannot be taken offline for patching.
- The long-term goal: **automate most triage and patching**, and develop new security architectures and coding standards.

The so what: **when a lab offers its best models to half the states in the union, it is not a PR move.** *(Source: [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/anthropic-cybersecurity-program-critical-infrastructure-open-source/))*

### 10. The catch nobody printed on the poster

I will not soften this, because the honesty rule cuts both ways.

- The **commercial terms are undisclosed**. As Axios noted, Anthropic has not said whether partners get free model access or who covers the compute costs.
- Patching industrial systems that cannot go offline remains an **unsolved problem**. A bad patch on a water plant is worse than the vulnerability.
- Anthropic's own verdict on its earlier effort: finding vulnerabilities has never been easier, but verification and repair is slow, people-dependent work, and Glasswing "has not yet cut cyber risk by enough."
- Anthropic expects AI to favor defenders **within two years**. Today, exploit costs keep falling.

The so what: **free scans are the easy part, and even Anthropic admits it.** *(Source: [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/08/anthropic-launches-critical-infrastructure-program-and-free-oss-scanner-for-open-source/))*

## How to start

**If you maintain an open-source project:** check whether your project meets the critical-impact bar, then submit the pull request to Anthropic's repo. Brace for raw, unreviewed reports. They will be fast and occasionally wrong.

**If you are a security-curious developer:** read the reproducer reports as free penetration-testing training. A working exploit with a bisection is a better teacher than any course.

**If you just ship software:** check whether your dependencies are enrolling. Free scanning only helps if maintainers actually apply the patches.

<u>Do not sleep on this because it is free. Free is the strategy.</u>

## The real pattern

The labs spent two years building models that find bugs faster than any human can, and that created a surplus of vulnerabilities nobody could process. So now they are building the other half of the pipeline for free, because a world full of AI-found exploits with no AI-speed patching is a world where their own models become the problem. The real pattern is simple: **whoever creates the surplus owns the cleanup.**

## Final thoughts

Free security from the people who made the insecurity. Take the scans. Patch fast.

And remember the uncomfortable bit: the same model that found the bug could have sold it.
