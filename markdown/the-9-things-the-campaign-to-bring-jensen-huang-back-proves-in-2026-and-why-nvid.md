# The 9 Things the Campaign to Bring Jensen Huang Back Proves in 2026, and Why Nvidia's Board Lost the Room

*He resigned on Thursday. By Sunday, Silicon Valley was running a war room to get him back.*

![Jensen Huang on stage in his black leather jacket, speaking at a keynote](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/beechrasta/medium-images/main/2026-11-16/jensen-huang-keynote-hero.jpg)

*Jensen Huang, on stage. Three days after he resigned, his allies were already organising his return. Credit: Forbes.*

Let's stop pretending **Nvidia's** board is in control of this story.

**Jensen Huang** resigned on Thursday, ending a 33 year run at the company he co-founded. By Sunday, more than 158,000 people had signed an open letter demanding he come back, **Elon Musk** had signed it, and **Huang** himself was reportedly discussing a return with confidants.

This article is not about the scandal. It is about what happened after the resignation, when the board discovered that firing the founder is the easy part.

## 1. The petition that broke the board's script

- An open letter at **standwithjensen.com** passed 100,000 signatures on Saturday and 158,000 by Sunday evening, and kept climbing past 164,000 that night.
- More than 7,500 signatories identified themselves as **Nvidia** employees, about 18% of the company's roughly 42,000 strong workforce.
- More than 80,000 identified themselves as shareholders.
- The signatures are self reported and unverified, and the campaign says it is not endorsed by **Nvidia** or **Huang** himself.

The letter asks the board to bring **Huang** back while the independent investigation runs, give him a chance to answer the allegations, and explain the decision with evidence. That is not a fan club. That is a governance demand, written in petition form.

**A board can ignore a hashtag. It cannot ignore a fifth of its own workforce.**

*(Source: [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/16/elon-musk-tech-execs-open-letter-jensen-huang-return-nvidia-ceo))*

## 2. The war room

- The **Financial Times** reported on Sunday that **Huang** has discussed a possible return with people close to him.
- One ally told the paper they were assembling a war room of Silicon Valley executives to lobby the board and the **White House**.
- The same ally said, and I quote, "Everyone that matters in tech outside the frontier labs loves Jensen," and that the efforts had **Huang's** support.
- **Nvidia** says no formal process for a return has begun.

Read that again. The ousted founder has a war room, a lobbying plan, and the **White House** on the target list, four days after resigning. The board has an interim CEO and a search committee.

**One side is running a campaign. The other side is running a process. Campaigns beat processes.**

*(Source: [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/16/elon-musk-tech-execs-open-letter-jensen-huang-return-nvidia-ceo))*

## 3. The tech heavyweights who signed, and the one who wrote the line everyone will quote

- **Elon Musk** signed on Friday, and said so publicly on **X**.
- **Tim Sweeney**, founder and CEO of **Epic Games**, signed. So did **Garry Tan**, president of **Y Combinator**, and **Amjad Masad**, CEO of **Replit**.
- **Travis Kalanick**, the **Uber** co-founder, wrote that **Huang** had "erred, yes, to err is to be human, but I do not believe anything he has done rises to the bar of separating him from his company."
- On Friday, major shareholders **Brad Gerstner** and **Gavin Baker** urged the board to keep "all options" open.

**Kalanick's** line is doing a lot of work, because it reframes the whole affair as a human mistake rather than a firing offence. That framing is the campaign's entire strategy in one sentence.

**When the richest men in tech start writing your defence for you, the board's silence gets very loud.**

*(Source: [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/16/elon-musk-tech-execs-open-letter-jensen-huang-return-nvidia-ceo))*

## 4. Bessent's 10 day ultimatum, and why the affair is not the real story

- Treasury Secretary **Scott Bessent** went on **Fox News** on Sunday and said **Nvidia** needs a "dramatic shake-up" and should hire its next CEO from outside the company.
- He wants answers within 10 days from the board and its outside counsel.
- His questions are not about the affair. They are about whether **Nvidia** knowingly allowed its most advanced AI chips to reach China.
- He wants to know whether the company deliberately ignored warnings that shell companies in Singapore and elsewhere were routing semiconductors to China, and whether its culture pushed sales that appeared unlawful in pursuit of revenue targets.

**Bessent** said it plainly: the compliance question matters more than the affair. The scandal got **Huang** fired. The chip question is what could get the company in real trouble.

**The affair is the tabloid story. The export controls are the national security story. Washington knows which one it cares about.**

*(Source: [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/16/elon-musk-tech-execs-open-letter-jensen-huang-return-nvidia-ceo))*

## 5. The H200 line in the sand

- The US forbids sales to China of the most cutting edge AI chips, as part of its strategy to stay ahead of **Beijing** in AI.
- In late 2025, **Trump** authorised sales of **Nvidia's H200** chips to China, but not the most advanced chips used to run the AI models reshaping modern society.
- **Bessent** said, "Nothing above the H200s can be sold to the Chinese," and called for an examination of "whether Nvidia had a culture of compliance around the US export restrictions."

This is the question that will decide **Nvidia's** next decade, and it has almost nothing to do with who the CEO is. If the investigation finds a culture that treated export controls as a suggestion, no CEO can fix that with a keynote.

**The line is drawn at the H200. Everything above it is the whole ballgame.**

*(Source: [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/16/elon-musk-tech-execs-open-letter-jensen-huang-return-nvidia-ceo))*

## 6. Megaspeed speaks, and the spy story gets murkier

- **Megaspeed**, the company linked to **Huang's** downfall, broke its silence on Monday, calling itself a "legitimate and lawful business."
- The company insists **Alice Huang**, accused of being a Chinese spy, left the business in late 2025.
- The espionage allegations against **Alice Huang**, who is not related to **Jensen Huang**, have yet to be conclusively established.
- **Huang** acknowledged the extramarital affair on November 8. The affair is confirmed. The spying is not.

This is the detail that makes the whole saga so combustible. The board acted on an affair with a woman *accused* of espionage, and the accusation itself remains unproven. The petition's core argument, that **Huang** was forced out without a fair hearing, feeds directly on that gap.

**A resignation built on an unproven accusation is a resignation that can be unbuilt.**

*(Source: [The Guardian, Megaspeed statement](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/16/megaspeed-company-breaks-slience-nvidia-jensen-huang-alice-huang))*

## 7. The board's problem: an investigation, a mob, and an earnings call

- **Colette Kress**, the CFO, is interim CEO. **Harvey C. Jones** is the board's first ever chair.
- Board chair **Jones** has publicly backed the interim leadership, saying the board "fully trusts" **Kress** and her team.
- **Nvidia** reports third quarter results on Tuesday, November 17. **Kress** will have to answer investors' questions about the succession, the investigation, and the export controls.
- **Nvidia** shares fell 13% last week, their biggest weekly drop since April 2025.

Tuesday is the deadline the board cannot move. **Kress** has to stand in front of investors and explain a company whose founder wants his job back, whose employees signed a petition, and whose biggest shareholder questions are about China, all while reporting the numbers from the quarter **Huang** ran.

**Earnings day does not care about your internal investigation timeline.**

*(Source: [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/114563-nearly-150000-people-sign-petition-requesting-jensen-huang.html))*

## 8. Not everyone inside Nvidia wants him back

- **Nvidia** vice president **Bryan Catanzaro** publicly rejected an internal letter circulating in support of **Huang** as fake.
- **Catanzaro** said he would not sign it and expressed full confidence in interim CEO **Kress**.
- The petition's employee and shareholder counts are self declared, and the campaign itself says they have not been independently verified.

This is the part the petition's supporters skip. The workforce is not unanimous. Some senior people inside the company are actively pushing back, and the board can point to them when it says the picture is complicated.

**A petition with 158,000 names is a movement. A movement with an internal opposition is a civil war.**

*(Source: [Coinotag, on the FT report and petition counts](https://en.coinotag.com/jensen-huang-nvidia-nvda-ceo-comeback-talks))*

## 9. What Tuesday decides, and what it does not

- The earnings report covers the last quarter **Huang** fully ran. The numbers will be strong, because **Nvidia's** business is strong.
- The call will be about everything except the numbers: the succession search, the investigation timeline, and **Bessent's** 10 day clock.
- Reported succession names include **Arm** CEO **Rene Haas**, **Lisa Su**, **Ian Buck** and Apple executive **Johny Srouji**.
- **Huang** and other **Nvidia** officials have agreed to testify before **Congress**, though no date has been set yet.

Here is the thing nobody on either side will say out loud: **Huang** does not hold a controlling stake, so a return needs board approval, and the board has given no sign it is considering one. The war room can lobby. The petition can grow. The board can still say no.

**The campaign can win the internet and lose the boardroom. That is the only outcome that matters, and it is still undecided.**

*(Source: [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/16/elon-musk-tech-execs-open-letter-jensen-huang-return-nvidia-ceo))*

## How to start

- **If you hold Nvidia stock:** watch Tuesday's call, not the petition counter. Listen for what **Kress** says about the investigation timeline and the export control answers.
- **If you work in tech:** notice how fast a founder's fate became a public campaign. Your company's next crisis will play out the same way, in public, in real time.
- **If you just follow the drama:** remember the two separate questions. Did **Huang** deserve to be fired for the affairs? And did **Nvidia** break export controls? They have different answers, and only the second one involves the government.

You do not need to pick a side. You need to keep the two questions separate, because everyone with an agenda is trying to merge them.

## The real pattern

Nvidia's board thought it was solving a scandal by removing a CEO. What it actually did was hand the story to everyone else: a petition, a war room, a Treasury secretary, and a founder who will not go quietly. The pattern is simple and brutal. In a company built around one person, the founder is not an employee you can fire. He is the product, the brand, and the shareholder base's identity, all at once. Remove him and you do not remove the problem. You become the problem.

## Final thoughts

Four days ago, **Jensen Huang** was the past. Today he is the frontrunner to be the future, and the board that fired him has not said a word about it. That silence is the loudest thing in tech right now. Tuesday will break it, one way or the other.
