# The 9 Things Jensen Huang's Spy Scandal Proves in 2026, and Why Nvidia Just Became a National Security Story

The CEO of the world's most valuable chipmaker admitted to an affair with a woman US intelligence calls a Chinese spy. The affair is confirmed. Everything else is a question nobody can answer yet.

Let's stop pretending this is a celebrity gossip story. It has an affair in it, sure, and the tabloid version writes itself. But the man at the center runs the company that makes the chips the entire AI race runs on.

On Sunday, November 8, **Jensen Huang**, the 63-year-old founder and CEO of **Nvidia**, emailed his employees and admitted he had been unfaithful to his wife. The same day, **Nvidia's board** announced an independent investigation into reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort.

This article is not about the affair. It is about what happens when the most important CEO in AI becomes a counterintelligence case study.

## 1. The email that said the quiet part out loud

- On Sunday, November 8, Huang sent a staff email that opened with an apology to his family and his colleagues.
- **"I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry."**
- He added that he had fallen short of his responsibility, promised to cooperate fully with the board's investigation, and said he was committed to earning back people's confidence.
- The board's statement never mentions the affair. Huang's email never mentions espionage. Each document is survivable alone. **Nvidia** published both on the same day.

*(Source: [CNET](https://www.cnet.com/news/nvidia-board-probes-reports-ceo-was-targeted-by-spy/))*

## 2. The board is investigating its own CEO, and backing him at the same time

- A committee of independent directors, assisted by outside counsel, will oversee the investigation into reporting that Huang "has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort."
- The board said it has **"no reason to believe"** that national security or **Nvidia** secrets were compromised, but that the inquiry will examine all available information.
- A spokesperson said the news will not slow **Nvidia** down and that Huang is committed to continuing to lead the company as CEO without interruption.
- The company told **Axios** he will keep running **Nvidia** while the investigation goes on. The directors are backing him and investigating him in the same statement.

*(Source: [NDTV Profit](https://www.ndtvprofit.com/markets/i-was-unfaithful-to-my-wife-jensen-huang-admits-affair-as-nvidia-board-opens-probe-chinese-spy-12301392))*

## 3. Who Alice Huang is, and why her job matters more than her name

- The woman is **Alice Huang**, also known as Huang Le. She is a Chinese citizen and a former senior executive at **Megaspeed**, a Singapore-based data center company. She is not related to Jensen Huang.
- **Megaspeed** buys **Nvidia** chips to run data centers that sell computing power to Chinese AI labs, and provides compute to **Alibaba**.
- The company split from a Chinese gaming firm in 2023 and builds data centers across Southeast Asia.
- Her job is the whole story. This is not about who she is. It is about what she bought, who she sold computing power to, and who she talked to while doing it.

*(Source: [CNET](https://www.cnet.com/news/nvidia-board-probes-reports-ceo-was-targeted-by-spy/))*

## 4. The story started with a chip-smuggling investigation, not a romance

- On October 29, the **New York Times** reported that US authorities were investigating Alice Huang, and that US intelligence agencies had her under electronic surveillance for allegedly facilitating the sale of advanced **Nvidia** chips to China in violation of US law.
- During that surveillance, officials discovered she was also allegedly funneling information to the Chinese government in Beijing.
- The trail started earlier. In late 2024, a US Commerce Department official visited a **Megaspeed** data center in Malaysia and noticed **Nvidia** chips sitting unopened in boxes, which fed suspicions the company was routing advanced American chips to China.
- Surveillance then picked up her communications with an American citizen, later identified as Jensen Huang through "unmasking," the process of revealing a US person's identity in intelligence reports.

*(Source: [CNET](https://www.cnet.com/news/nvidia-board-probes-reports-ceo-was-targeted-by-spy/))*

## 5. The November 6 memo changed the story from smuggling to spying

- On November 6, the **New York Times** reported that a US intelligence memo says China's **Ministry of State Security** recruited Alice Huang to watch **Nvidia's** CEO.
- Her brief reportedly included pushing Huang to lobby Washington for looser limits on AI chip sales to China.
- The **Financial Times** reported the same recruitment claim, and the **Times** reported that Secretary of State **Marco Rubio** drafted a memo warning top Trump administration officials about the relationship. Asked about it on Sunday, Rubio declined to comment.
- Here is the part everyone skips: **the memo is not public**, so nobody outside the government can test its sources. The recruitment claim is an intelligence assessment, not a court finding.

*(Source: [The State of AI](https://www.thestateofai.com/news/nvidia-jensen-huang-affair-board-investigation-china-spy))*

## 6. Megaspeed, the billion-dollar customer at the center of it

- **Megaspeed** bought more than **$4.8 billion** worth of **Nvidia** chips over the past two years, according to the **New York Times**.
- The US Commerce Department has accused the company since 2024 of smuggling **$2 billion** of advanced silicon to China.
- **Nvidia's** defense is specific. The company says it reviewed every GPU shipped to **Megaspeed** and found no evidence any had been illegally diverted to China, and that Huang's interactions with her were normal customer meetings.
- **Megaspeed** has hired outside lawyers to run its own internal investigation. Singapore's Ministry of Home Affairs said on October 31 that it never investigated her alleged intelligence ties, and that its probe targets **Megaspeed**, not Alice Huang personally.

*(Source: [AAP](https://aapnews.aap.com.au/news/nvidia-to-probe-ceo-s-links-with-alleged-chinese-spy))*

## 7. Congress wants Huang in a closed room on November 18

- On November 4, three House committee chairs wrote to Huang asking him to attend a closed-door briefing on November 18 about his interactions with Alice Huang and **Nvidia's** safeguards against foreign espionage.
- The three are **John Moolenaar** of the Select Committee on China, **Brian Mast** of the Foreign Affairs Committee, and **Rick Crawford** of the Intelligence Committee.
- Mast has clashed with **Nvidia** over export controls before. In January this year, he accused **Nvidia** of lobbying hard to sell millions of chips to Chinese companies and challenged Huang to debate the issue on Fox News.
- A closed-door briefing means no cameras, no transcript, and a lot of leaks afterward. Plan accordingly.

*(Source: [NDTV Profit](https://www.ndtvprofit.com/markets/i-was-unfaithful-to-my-wife-jensen-huang-admits-affair-as-nvidia-board-opens-probe-chinese-spy-12301392))*

## 8. The export-control maze that made all of this possible

- In 2022, the Biden administration banned sales of a series of advanced **Nvidia** chips to China. In 2025, President **Donald Trump** relaxed those rules, and reports show illegal smuggling operations continue anyway.
- The current compromise is the **H200**, an older Hopper-generation chip sold to approved Chinese customers under case-by-case licenses, with **25% of the revenue** flowing to the US government.
- About **600,000 H200 chips** are stockpiled in Hong Kong awaiting Beijing's import approval, according to a November 5 report, and China-bound H200 sales were under **1% of Nvidia's data-center revenue** last quarter.
- **Nvidia's** own outlook now assumes zero China data-center compute revenue going forward. The company took a **$400 million** write-down on unsold H200 inventory.
- Enforcement is real but leaky. In March 2026, US prosecutors charged two **Super Micro** executives and a broker in an alleged scheme involving about **$2.5 billion** in server purchases, with at least **$510 million** worth of servers allegedly diverted to China.

*(Source: [Kalkine](https://kalkine.ca/news/technology/what-keeps-about-600000-nvidia-nasdaqnvda-h200-chips-in-hong-kong-waiting-on-beijing))*

## 9. What nobody has actually proven yet

- **No charges** have been announced against Jensen Huang, Alice Huang, **Nvidia**, or **Megaspeed** staff. Nobody has accused Huang of a crime or of sharing secrets.
- China's foreign ministry calls the reports a baseless smear.
- **Nvidia** previously described Huang's interactions with Alice Huang as not unusual or unexpected, given that he talks with thousands of customer executives every year.
- The board says nothing was compromised. Its investigation exists to prove that.
- <u>So here is the honest scoreboard: the affair is admitted, the espionage is alleged, and the gap between those two words is the entire story.</u>

*(Source: [The State of AI](https://www.thestateofai.com/news/nvidia-jensen-huang-affair-board-investigation-china-spy))*
