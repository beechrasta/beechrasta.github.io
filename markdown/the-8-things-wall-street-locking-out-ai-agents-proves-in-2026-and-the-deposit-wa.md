# The 8 Things Wall Street Locking Out AI Agents Proves in 2026, and the Deposit War Behind It

*The biggest banks in America just told your AI agent it cannot come in. The excuse is security. The reason is money. Both are true.*

Let's stop pretending the AI agent revolution will arrive politely and knock on the front door.

**The biggest banks in America just bolted that door.** And in the same week, the web itself proved their fear is not paranoia.

This article is not a list of bank policies. It is about who gets to move your money when the thing moving it is not you.

## 1. Jamie Dimon said no first, and he said it out loud

- On **JPMorgan Chase's** October 13 earnings call, CEO **Jamie Dimon** said the bank would not allow outside agents inside, because it is too risky for clients and for **JPMorgan** itself.
- His phrase was **"rogue agents"**, and he warned there could be thousands of them.
- A **JPMorgan** spokeswoman confirmed the block covers any outside agent logging in with a customer's credentials to pay bills or move money.
- **Read-only access through open banking connectors like Plaid still works.** The ban is on agents acting, not agents looking.

**The so what: the most powerful banker alive drew the line in public, on an earnings call, so every other bank had cover to follow.**

*(Source: [Techtember](https://techtember.news/article/citi-bofa-block-ai-agents-october-17/))*

## 2. Within days, the whole street followed

- **Citigroup** told the **Financial Times** on October 16 that it does not permit third-party AI agents or assistants to log into customer accounts or transact directly on its platforms.
- **Bank of America** restricted agents' access to consumer deposit accounts while it works out who is liable for payments an AI approves.
- **Charles Schwab**, **Morgan Stanley**, and **Wells Fargo** have all moved to block or restrict unauthorized agents, according to reporting.
- **Amazon** had already shut autonomous assistants out of its own storefront, in the same wave.

**The so what: this was not one cautious bank. It was an industry moving in concert, in under a week.**

*(Source: [ET Now](https://www.etnownews.com/technology/citi-and-bank-of-america-block-ai-agents-from-customer-accounts-following-amazons-latest-move-article-156363385))*

## 3. Here is the awkward part: there was no hack

- The banks have **not reported a hack**, and no customer money has been publicly confirmed lost because of an AI agent.
- What actually happened is much more boring, and much more important: **customers handed their own passwords to their own agents**, and the agents logged in as them.
- **David Sacks**, the White House AI czar, called that a possible breach of bank terms, but said it is "in no way" a cyberattack.
- His distinction matters: **a user-authorized agent is not a rogue hacker**, even when it breaks the bank's rules.

**The so what: the banks are not fighting criminals here. They are fighting their own customers' choices.**

*(Source: [Demokratis](https://demokratis.co.id/en/banks-restrict-third-party-ai-agents))*

## 4. Follow the deposits, because that is where the real panic lives

- Behind the security argument sits roughly **$1.6 trillion in deposits that pay no interest**, money customers leave sitting in checking accounts.
- **Apollo** chief economist **Torsten Slok** warned that agents could automatically sweep idle household cash into accounts paying 3.3 percent to 5 percent a year, against a national average of 0.1 percent for US checking accounts.
- If every household did that, banks could lose much of the cheap deposit base their lending runs on, at an estimated extra cost of **$47 billion a year**.
- The timing is not a coincidence: the crackdown followed **Meta's** release of its personal AI assistant, **Muse**, in September, an agent built to act on your behalf.

**The so what: <u>an agent that moves your money to a better rate is not a security threat to you. It is a business model threat to your bank.</u>**

*(Source: [ET Now](https://www.etnownews.com/technology/citi-and-bank-of-america-block-ai-agents-from-customer-accounts-following-amazons-latest-move-article-156363385))*

## 5. Nobody knows who pays when the agent gets it wrong

- **Bank of America** CEO **Brian Moynihan** said there should be "a human in the loop" to authorize money leaving an account, at least until the technology reduces the risk of mistakes.
- **Dimon** warned customers who hand an agent their password that "there's a liability transfer", meaning the bank may not cover you.
- **Senator Mark Warner** said consumer AI agents touching bank accounts should carry "fiduciary-like duties of care", a legal duty to act in your interest, and pointed to his proposed **AI AGENT Act**.
- No new law has passed yet, so right now the liability question is answered by each bank's terms of service, not by statute.

**The so what: until the law says who eats an agent's mistake, every bank will default to no.**

*(Source: [TechCheatCodes](https://techcheatcodes.com/news/2026/banks-block-ai-agents-accounts/))*

## 6. Then PHP2Shell proved the fear is not theoretical

- On October 21, the **PHP** team shipped emergency patches for two critical flaws, **CVE-2026-109618** and **CVE-2026-109619**, collectively called **PHP2Shell**.
- Both scored **9.2 out of 10** in severity, and either one could let an unauthenticated attacker run code on a vulnerable web server.
- **PHP** runs roughly 70 percent of websites, including every **WordPress** site, so the exposure was planetary.
- Security firm **Calif** found the flaw with a large language model on August 1, working with **OpenAI** and **Wiz**, and **Amazon** spotted an exploit attempt on October 17, four days before public disclosure.
- **Wikipedia** took systems offline as a precaution, the **Internet Archive** went dark, and government sites across Europe went offline while operators patched.

**The so what: <u>AI found a flaw hiding in the web for over a decade, and attackers were exploiting it before the patch existed. The banks watched that week and thought: now imagine that power logged into a checking account.</u>**

*(Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_October_security_incidents))*

## 7. AI is both the arsonist and the fire department now

- The same class of technology the banks fear is the technology that found **PHP2Shell** in the first place.
- **Wiz** researchers said frontier AI models lowered the barrier to turning an old flaw into a working exploit.
- **Bank of America** disclosed it hired 1,000 contractors to fix software flaws discovered by frontier AI models.
- So the street is simultaneously using AI to find bugs, and banning AI from touching money, in the same month.

**The so what: the argument was never "AI is unsafe." The argument is "AI is powerful, and power near money needs rules first."**

*(Source: [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/black-october-php-vulnerability-cybersecurity-ai-threat-2026-10))*

## 8. What this means if you build agents

- **Stop building on screen scraping and shared passwords.** That is the exact pattern every major bank just banned, and your users will pay the liability price.
- **Build on permissioned rails instead:** open banking connectors, read-only data access, and approved provider APIs that never see a password.
- **Put a human approval step on every money movement**, not as a popup annoyance but as the product: the agent proposes, the human approves, the receipt is auditable.
- **Design for the fiduciary standard now**, before the law forces it: act in the user's interest, show your work, and never move money silently.

**The so what: the agent economy will be built on delegated permission, not borrowed passwords. The banks just told you the architecture.**

## How to start

- **If you are a builder:** audit one agent flow today and replace every password-based login with an approved connector. That is your moat now.
- **If you are an everyday user:** never hand your main bank password to an agent. Use a separate account with limited funds for anything automated.
- **If you are a founder:** your pitch is no longer "our agent does everything." It is "our agent does everything with receipts."

## The real pattern

The banks are not wrong about security, and they are not innocent about deposits. Both things are true at once, and the industry that wins will be the one that solves both: permissioned access that protects the customer and still lets their money work for them.

## Final thoughts

Your agent will move your money one day. The question is whether it does it with your bank's blessing, with your explicit approval, and with someone liable when it errs. Until then, keep your password to yourself, and let the agent earn its keys one permission at a time.
