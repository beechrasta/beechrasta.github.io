## 4. The safety setup, honestly

- A **Sentinel agent** approves internet-bound actions. There is a full **audit trail** of everything it did and why.
- **Nothing gets published, sent, or spent without my approval.** That rule is load-bearing. It is the reason I trust it with my inbox and my accounts.
- Now the catch, stated plainly: **Meta trains on chats by default unless you switch it off in settings.** Say it plainly: the writer who mentions the catch earns the trust.
- I switched it off. It took ten seconds. I think everyone should know the toggle exists instead of discovering it in a headline.

### The privacy part nobody wants to write

- The audit trail and approvals are about what the agent *does*. They say nothing about what Meta *does with what it reads*. Different question, different answer.
- So let's be blunt: **Meta will most likely train on your data.** The default is on, the toggle is buried in settings, and a trillion-dollar ad company does not hand out a frontier agent out of charity. *Assume your chats are training material unless you opt out. And honestly, opt out anyway.*
- **Nothing here is end-to-end encrypted.** Not the agent chat, not the files it reads, not the memory it keeps of you. If it can read it to help you, it can read it for other purposes too. *That is just how the architecture works.*
- Now ask the uncomfortable question: why are the usage limits so generous? Why is the free tier this good? **Because the product being trained is not just the model. It is your habits.** Generous limits get you to hand over your inbox, your calendar, your notes, your entire workflow. The stickier the agent, the richer the data.
- And Meta offers no real guarantee about that data beyond a settings toggle. No cryptographic proof, no zero-knowledge anything. Just trust, plus a checkbox.
- I still use it every day. I still think it is worth it. But I use it with my eyes open, and so should you.

The "so what": the safety design is genuinely good, and the privacy reality is genuinely worth knowing. **Both things are true at once.**
