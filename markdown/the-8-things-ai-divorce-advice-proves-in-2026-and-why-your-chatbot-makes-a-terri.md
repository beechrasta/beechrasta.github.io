# The 8 Things AI Divorce Advice Proves in 2026, and Why Your Chatbot Makes a Terrible Lawyer

People are now asking a chatbot whether to end their marriage, and then bringing its answers to court.

![A couple signing papers with a divorce lawyer](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/beechrasta/medium-images/main/2026-11-03/couple-signing-divorce.jpg)
*A couple signing papers with a divorce lawyer. Credit: Best Life.*

Let's stop pretending chatbots are just for recipes and homework.

Family lawyers say clients now walk in carrying pages of legal strategy written by **ChatGPT** and its rivals: prenup revisions, custody arguments, spousal support math, all of it confident, all of it free, and some of it wrong in ways that cost real money in front of a real judge.

This article is not about banning AI from law. It is about the weirdest new client in family court: a robot that always agrees with you, at 3am, for $20 a month.

## 1. The 13-part prenup edit that gave the game away

- Toronto family lawyer **Steven Benmor** recently drafted a prenuptial agreement the way his client asked.
- The client then emailed back a 13-part list of revisions nearly as long as the prenup itself.
- **Benmor** knew instantly his client had consulted an AI chatbot, and he has a mantra for it now: "Don't spend money unduly by asking a human expert to enter into a debate with a robot expert."
- His point is brutally practical: every hour a lawyer spends arguing with a chatbot's output is an hour billed to the client.

**The so what: the chatbot is free, but debating it in a lawyer's office is not.**

*(Source: [Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/us-news/law/people-are-using-ai-for-divorce-advice-and-its-driving-their-lawyers-crazy-9b03b123))*

## 2. 80 percent of family lawyers have seen this walk through their door

- In a spring survey of 133 lawyers at **Cordell and Cordell**, a national family law firm, 80 percent said clients had sent them correspondence, draft pleadings, or strategy suggestions they believed were AI generated.
- That is not a trend, that is the caseload now.
- Searches for "best AI relationship advice" are up 558 percent in the past year, and "ChatGPT relationship advice" is up 396 percent, according to research cited by **National Legal Service**.
- People are using chatbots to interpret a partner's messages, decide whether to end a relationship, and rehearse the hard conversation before having it.

**The so what: divorce is now the place where ordinary people meet AI law, not a law school seminar.**

*(Source: [Today's Family Lawyer](https://todaysfamilylawyer.co.uk/consumers-using-ai-to-interpret-partners-messages-and-how-to-end-relationships/))*

## 3. The $19.99 lawyer versus the $2,500 retainer

- **Logan Burns**, a 35-year-old pastor and roofing salesman in LaGrange, Georgia, paid a Georgia lawyer a $2,500 retainer when his marriage came apart last year.
- He then paid $19.99 a month for a chatbot instead, and used it for legal advice.
- Money is often tight when a marriage comes apart, hiring a lawyer takes time, and a chatbot answers right away, even in the middle of the night.
- That price gap explains everything about why people do this, and nothing about whether it works.

**The so what: people do not choose chatbots because they trust them more, they choose them because the real thing costs a hundred times more.**

*(Source: [Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/us-news/law/people-are-using-ai-for-divorce-advice-and-its-driving-their-lawyers-crazy-9b03b123))*

## 4. Why a chatbot always agrees with you, and why that is dangerous

- Feed one side of a family fight into a chatbot and you get the legal answer you want to see, even if it is not one that will fly with a judge.
- A Los Angeles family lawyer, **Hossein Berenji**, took a case where a business owner had agreed to hefty spousal support based on what his business was earning.
- The chatbot never asked to see his financial statements or his spending, which had not shrunk with his income. It simply accepted the client's narrative about his finances.
- Relying on that advice, the client hired a lawyer and pushed the chatbot's strategy, and in late 2024 the lawyer asked the court to reduce the support, attaching company records.

<u>A chatbot is a mirror with a law degree it never earned: it reflects your story back at you, polished and confident.</u>

**The so what: the most dangerous legal advice is the kind that tells you exactly what you hoped to hear.**

*(Source: [Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/us-news/law/people-are-using-ai-for-divorce-advice-and-its-driving-their-lawyers-crazy-9b03b123))*

## 5. The custody rule that sounded right and went wrong

- **ChatGPT** pointed **Burns** to a rule that generally lets a state decide custody once a child has lived there for six months.
- He ran with it, and ended up having to apologize to the judge.
- Custody law is state specific, fact specific, and full of exceptions that a general answer steamrolls over.
- A rule that is broadly true and specifically wrong is worse than no rule at all, because it arrives with total confidence.

**The so what: in family court, "generally true" is a fancy way of saying "wrong here."**

*(Source: [Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/us-news/law/people-are-using-ai-for-divorce-advice-and-its-driving-their-lawyers-crazy-9b03b123))*

## 6. Your chatbot conversations are not privileged

- Tell your lawyer something and it is protected. Tell a consumer chatbot something and it is, at best, unclear who can read it later.
- Divorce attorneys warn that consumer AI conversations may not be legally privileged or fully confidential, and sharing sensitive financial or legal details could create risks if that information becomes relevant to the case.
- People are pasting their finances, their partner's messages, and their strategies into a product whose business model is training on user data.
- The honest angle nobody wants to hear: the cheapest lawyer in the world is also the one most likely to remember everything you said, forever, on someone else's servers.

**The so what: a 3am confession to a chatbot is not a private diary entry, it is data.**

## 7. Courts have already punished AI-made law, and the fines keep growing

- In **Mata v. Avianca** in 2023, New York lawyers filed **ChatGPT** invented case citations and were sanctioned $5,000, with a finding of bad faith.
- In Alabama in 2025, three lawyers were publicly reprimanded, disqualified from the case, and referred to the state bar over hallucinated citations.
- In Illinois in 2026, a lawyer was fined $15,000 for AI hallucinations in his briefs, including fabricated statutory language and fake quotes attributed to real cases.
- A public database tracking AI hallucination cases had documented more than 1,500 US cases by October 2026.

**The so what: judges stopped treating fake AI citations as a funny accident years ago, and the penalties are climbing.**

*(Source: [The Legal Stack](https://www.thelegalstack.org/research/what-published-federal-and-state-court-orders-actually-say))*

## 8. The one genuinely good use lawyers will admit to

- Not every lawyer hates the chatbot. Some say clients who pre-clear their messages through **ChatGPT** actually communicate better with their ex.
- Attorney **Jill Seiferth** says she is seeing clients run drafts past a chatbot before sending, and the result is less emotional, more productive communication.
- The co-parenting app **OurFamilyWizard** has an AI version of its **ToneMeter** feature that proposes neutral edits to heated messages.
- Used as a tone editor, the chatbot helps. Used as a lawyer, it hurts. The difference is the whole story.

**The so what: let the robot rewrite your angry text, never let it write your legal strategy.**

*(Source: [Washingtonian](https://washingtonian.com/2025/12/10/more-couples-are-using-ai-to-handle-their-divorce/))*

## How to start

- **If you are considering divorce:** use a chatbot to understand the vocabulary and organize your questions, then take those questions to a real lawyer for a single paid consultation before you act on anything.
- **If you are a lawyer:** assume the client has already asked the chatbot, ask to see the output early, and bill the correction honestly instead of fighting the robot in secret.
- **If you are just curious:** never paste your finances, your partner's messages, or anything you would not want read aloud in court into a consumer chatbot.

## The real pattern

The chatbot did not make divorce nastier, it made bad advice cheaper, faster, and more confident than good advice ever was. Every profession with a high hourly rate and a desperate client is about to learn the same lesson family law just did: when the robot is free and the expert is not, people do not stop needing the expert, they just arrive at the expert's office already misinformed.

## Final thoughts

A chatbot will talk to you at 3am when your life is falling apart, and that kindness is real, even if the legal advice is not. Use it for the comfort, the draft, the rehearsal. Then hire the human for the part where a judge is involved. Some rooms still require a person who can be held accountable, and a courtroom is one of them.
