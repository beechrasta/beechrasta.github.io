The 9 Things a 385 Dollar Raspberry Pi Proves in 2026, and Why AI Just Ate the Cheapest Computer on Earth

The computer that taught a generation to code now costs more than a budget laptop, and the reason is sitting in a data center a thousand miles away.

A Raspberry Pi board up close. The 16GB model now costs $385, more than three times its $120 launch price. Photo by Jainath Ponnala on Unsplash.

Let's stop pretending this is a price hike. A price hike is when a company charges more because it can. This is something else: the cheapest computer on earth just got priced out of its own mission by the AI boom.

On November 2, Raspberry Pi raised the price of its top 16GB Raspberry Pi 5 by $80, to $385. It launched at $120 in January 2025. That is more than three times the launch price, in under two years, for the exact same board.

This article is not a shopping guide. It is about what happens when the AI industry starts eating the parts that everybody else needs to build anything at all.

1. The number is 385 dollars, the board is the same board

  • The 16GB Raspberry Pi 5 now lists at $385, up from $305 overnight on November 2.
  • It launched in January 2025 at $120. Same board, same chip, same ports. Nothing changed except the price tag.
  • The jump is 221 percent in less than two years, and this was the fourth increase on this one model.

You are paying $265 extra for a computer that did not get $265 better.

(Source: The Register)

2. The price ladder has no plateau in sight

  • January 2025: launches at $120.
  • December 2025: up to $145, the first rise.
  • February 2026: up to $205, another $60.
  • April 2026: up to $305, a $100 jump in one go.
  • November 2026: up to $385, another $80.

Four hikes in 22 months, and each one was bigger than the last one felt. The company calls every single one temporary.

A temporary increase that happens four times is not temporary, it is a trend with good PR.

(Source: TechTimes)

3. AI data centers are eating the RAM

  • The memory inside a Pi 5 is LPDDR4X, the same family of mobile memory that goes into mid-range phones and IoT gadgets.
  • Samsung formally discontinued LPDDR4 and LPDDR4X production in April 2026, converting its lines to LPDDR5 and high-bandwidth memory for AI accelerators.
  • SK Hynix wound down its LPDDR4 volume production in the first half of 2026 for the same reason.
  • In April, Raspberry Pi said the price of the LPDDR4 memory in its boards had risen sevenfold in a year.

The AI boom does not just need GPUs. It needs memory, vast amounts of it, and the memory makers would rather sell to a data center buying by the shipping container than to a hobby board maker buying by the reel.

Your $35 computer and a $100 billion data center are now bidding for the same chips, and guess who wins.

(Source: TechTimes)

4. There is exactly one supplier left, and it knows it

  • For the 16GB model, Micron supplies a single package with eight memory dies stacked inside it.
  • CEO Eben Upton blamed the latest increase on "a lack of alternative supply for 16GB DRAM, compounded by other ongoing component shortages."
  • Raspberry Pi has spent 2026 trying to qualify additional memory suppliers, and so far there is no second source for the dense chip this board needs.
  • Micron has previously warned that memory shortages could drag on into 2028 or beyond.

One supplier for the most important part of your flagship product is not a supply chain. It is a hostage situation with a polite press release.

When the only company that can sell you the chip raises its price, your only move is to pass it on or stop selling the board.

(Source: The Register)

5. The extra 8GB now costs more than a whole board

  • The 8GB Raspberry Pi 5 lists at $175. The 16GB model lists at $385.
  • That means stepping from 8GB to 16GB costs $210, which is more than a complete 8GB Pi 5.
  • The 4GB model is $110, the 2GB is $77.50, the 1GB is $45.
  • The 16GB Compute Module 5, the version companies build into industrial products, also went up $40, to $375 and $380 for the wireless version.

This is the number that breaks people's brains. The RAM upgrade costs more than the computer.

When the memory costs more than the machine, the machine is no longer the product. The memory is.

(Source: HWBusters)

A processor held by tweezers, showing its gold pins. Memory chips like these are the reason the Pi keeps getting more expensive. Photo by Brian Kostiuk on Unsplash.

6. The keyboard computer escaped, and that tells you everything

  • The Raspberry Pi 500+, a full desktop computer built into a mechanical keyboard, also carries 16GB of RAM.
  • It kept its price at $485. It was spared this round entirely.
  • Lower-memory boards were also untouched this time, though the 2GB Pi 4 and Pi 5 were repriced in October 2026.
  • Upton has advised buyers to pick models with less RAM, repurpose older boards, or optimize their software to cope with tighter constraints.

Read that advice again. The CEO of Raspberry Pi is telling you to buy less computer and write tighter code. That is a company managing a shortage, not a company selling you a dream.

The 500+ surviving tells you the company protects the products it can still afford to make, and rations the ones it cannot.

(Source: PCWorld)

7. The people who get hurt most are the ones the Pi was built for

  • The Pi was built for classrooms, students, and hobbyists, the people with the least money and the most curiosity.
  • A $385 bare board still needs a case, a power supply, and storage before it does anything. The real cost of a working setup is now well past $450.
  • In India, where a Pi is often a student's first Linux machine and first server, that price puts it next to a used laptop, and a used laptop wins that fight.
  • Schools and coding clubs that budget per student just lost a third of their fleet to a memory market they never signed up for.

The Pi democratized computing because it was cheap enough to break. At this price, breaking one hurts, and the kids who learn by breaking things are exactly the ones who stop trying.

The AI boom is supposed to make computing cheaper for everyone. Right now it is making the cheapest computer in the world unaffordable.

(Source: PC Guide)

8. The industrial users are quietly getting hit too

  • The Compute Module 5 is the version companies solder into real products, kiosks, factory controllers, medical devices.
  • Its 16GB variants went up $40 each in the same announcement.
  • Industrial buyers cannot just switch to the 8GB model the way a hobbyist can. Their software was validated on 16GB, and revalidating costs more than the price hike.
  • Raspberry Pi says meaningful new memory supply is not expected until 2028, and the next generation board, the Pi 6, is not expected before early 2028 either.

Hobbyists complain on forums. Industrial buyers quietly redesign their products around a different board, or eat the margin, or pass it to their customers. Either way, the cost lands on someone who never asked for an AI data center.

The hobbyist story is the loud one. The industrial story is the expensive one.

(Source: Circuit Digest)

9. How to buy a Pi in 2026 without getting scalped

  • Buy only from approved resellers. Raspberry Pi has warned that resellers are charging far above the official price or selling products that do not match their listings.
  • If you do not genuinely need 16GB, buy the 8GB model at $175. For most home servers, media centers, and learning projects, it is the same experience.
  • Dig out the old Pi 4 in your drawer before buying anything. A used board you already own beats a new board at triple the price.
  • Wait if you can. Upton insists the increases are temporary and promises to roll them back when memory costs fall, and he has kept that promise before, but Micron says shortages could last into 2028.

Nobody is saying never buy a Pi. I am saying do not pay scalper prices for a board whose own maker says the price is a hostage to a memory market that has not even peaked yet.

The smartest Pi purchase in 2026 might be the one you do not make.

(Source: Talk with Tech)

Hands wiring electronics at a workbench. Makers are the people the Pi was built for, and they are the ones paying the new price. Photo by Eric Stoynov on Unsplash.

How to start

  • If you are a student: grab the 8GB Pi 5 or a second-hand Pi 4. Your first Linux server does not need 16GB of RAM, it needs you to break it and fix it.
  • If you are a maker: audit what your projects actually use. Most home automation and media server builds sit under 4GB. Buy the RAM you use, not the RAM you fantasize about.
  • If you are a teacher: budget per student at the 8GB price and teach memory-efficient code as a feature, not a compromise. Your students will be better engineers for it.
  • If you build products on the Compute Module: start qualifying an alternative board now, before the next increase. The next one is coming, and 2028 is a long time to wait for relief.

The real pattern

The Raspberry Pi was never really about the board. It was proof that computing could be cheap enough for anyone, and the AI boom just proved the opposite, that when the richest industry on earth wants the same parts, cheap computing is the first thing that gets priced out. The lesson is not that AI is evil. The lesson is that every technology has a supply chain, and the supply chain always bills the smallest customer first.

Final thoughts

A $385 Raspberry Pi is still a wonderful computer. It is just no longer a cheap one, and cheap was the whole point. The next time someone tells you AI will make everything more affordable, ask them what happened to the RAM. The answer is sitting on a shelf, in a bare board, at three times its launch price, waiting for a memory market that may not come back until 2028.
