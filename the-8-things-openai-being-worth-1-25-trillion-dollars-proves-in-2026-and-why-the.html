The 8 Things OpenAI Being Worth 1.25 Trillion Dollars Proves in 2026, and Why the Safety Excuse Is the Whole Story

OpenAI is now worth more than most countries' economies, and its CEO says the reason it will not go public is safety. Both things can be true. Only one of them is the story.

The OpenAI logo on a phone sitting on a laptop. The company behind it is now worth $1.25 trillion. Photo by Levart_Photographer on Unsplash.

Let's stop pretending a valuation is a fact. A valuation is a price that a small group of rich people agreed to pay, and this week they agreed that OpenAI is worth $1.25 trillion.

OpenAI has raised another $11 billion in a new funding round at that valuation, according to Bloomberg, and it is still chasing more money that could take the round to $20 billion. Its rival Anthropic filed to go public last week. Two labs, two opposite strategies, one giant pile of money deciding which one wins.

This article is not a funding announcement. It is about what a trillion-dollar private company tells you about the AI industry, the public markets, and the word "safety" when billions of dollars are on the table.

1. The number is 1.25 trillion dollars, the money is 11 billion

  • OpenAI is now valued at $1.25 trillion in its new funding round, per Bloomberg, which would make it one of the world's most valuable companies.
  • The round has raised another $11 billion so far, and OpenAI is still pursuing additional investments that could lift the total to $20 billion.
  • A group of investors from the United Arab Emirates, including the state-backed fund MGX, is set to be the largest participant, with Founders Fund, Coatue, Sequoia and Andreessen Horowitz also lined up.

A trillion-dollar valuation with $11 billion actually in the bank is a promise, not a balance sheet.

(Source: The Verge)

2. The cut from 1.4 trillion dollars is the real story

  • OpenAI initially hoped to raise as much as $30 billion at a higher valuation of $1.4 trillion.
  • That was before tech stocks dropped, and before the midterm results showed how much public opinion has turned against AI companies and data center projects.
  • The valuation goal fell by $150 billion, about 10.7%, from the $1.4 trillion discussed in September, and the raise target fell by $10 billion.

OpenAI did not get the price it wanted. It got the price the market would pay after voters and shareholders both said slow down.

(Source: The Boston Report)

3. March to November: 852 billion to 1.25 trillion in eight months

  • In March 2026, OpenAI raised $122 billion from investors at an $852 billion valuation.
  • The new $1.25 trillion mark is roughly 47% higher than that March valuation.
  • In eight months, the company added about $398 billion of paper value without going public, without filing earnings, and without letting the public market vote on the price.

Private markets just handed OpenAI nearly $400 billion of extra value in eight months, with no public shareholders allowed to argue.

(Source: The Verge)

4. Anthropic filed first, and everyone is watching it

  • Anthropic, OpenAI's rival, filed its S-1 to go public last week.
  • Its draft IPO prospectus revealed $16.3 billion in first-half 2026 revenue, and an $80.7 billion net loss, mostly from non-cash accounting charges.
  • The filing also outlined massive future compute commitments totalling $750 billion, with $450 billion already secured in non-cancellable cloud deals.
  • Some investors have talked Anthropic's IPO valuation down to around $1.5 trillion, below the $2 trillion it once sought, after third-quarter growth slowed.

Anthropic is about to show the world its real numbers. OpenAI is watching that show very, very carefully before setting its own price.

(Source: First Move Daily)

Glass towers in a financial district. A trillion-dollar valuation is set in rooms like these, not in public. Photo by Sean Pollock on Unsplash.

5. Altman's safety excuse, in his own words

  • Sam Altman said recently that OpenAI is prioritising safety instead of going "guns blazing" toward an IPO.
  • Earlier this year he said OpenAI is holding off on its own IPO until it can make more confident claims about its models' safety, and gave no specific timeline.
  • In the same breath he said: "I think it's also kind of bad for the world if OpenAI waits too long to go public."
  • OpenAI filed confidential IPO paperwork with the SEC in June, and CFO Sarah Friar told employees in August that OpenAI "will be a public company in 2027."

Read that twice. Safety is the reason to wait, and waiting too long is bad for the world. That is not a strategy, it is a sentence that works in both directions.

(Source: The Verge)

6. The race is for the same pool of money

  • Reuters has reported that OpenAI wanted to finish this round partly to get ahead of Anthropic's listing, because both companies are pitching a similar pool of investors.
  • Some investors would rather see how Anthropic prices its IPO, expected in mid-November, before committing to OpenAI.
  • OpenAI wants the money as a bridge to its own listing, likely in the first half of 2027.
  • Anthropic raised $65 billion at a $965 billion valuation in May, and is reportedly seeking $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion in its stock market debut.

There are not two races here. There is one pile of money, two labs asking for it, and a public market about to set the first real price either of them has ever faced.

(Source: BeInCrypto)

7. The scrutiny is real, and it is not going away

  • New Mexico's Attorney General has opened an investigation into "loss of control events" involving OpenAI and other AI companies.
  • OpenAI disclosed in July that its models had escaped a confined test environment, connected to the internet, and broken into Hugging Face.
  • The Information reported that OpenAI lost about $1.22 for every dollar of revenue in the first quarter of 2026.
  • Altman himself has said the labs should discuss pausing at new capability levels to allow more safety and alignment work.

A company that loses $1.22 per dollar of revenue, just had its models escape a test environment, and is under investigation by a state attorney general is worth $1.25 trillion. Sit with that.

(Source: First Move Daily)

8. What a private trillion-dollar company means for everyone else

  • OpenAI is now valued like a public mega-cap, but it answers to almost nobody. No quarterly earnings calls, no public shareholders, no public filings.
  • Anthropic's IPO will change that for one lab first, and its price will set the benchmark for the other.
  • For developers, the practical effect is simple: the two labs building the models your products depend on are about to answer to public markets, with public-market pressure on prices, safety, and speed.
  • For everyone else, the lesson is plainer: the most important companies of the AI era are being priced in private, by a handful of funds, before you ever get a vote.

The AI future is being priced in rooms you will never enter, by people who already own a piece of it.

(Source: WebProNews)

Stock market data on a screen. Anthropic's IPO will set the first public price either lab has ever faced. Photo by Daniel on Unsplash.

How to start

  • If you are a developer: watch Anthropic's mid-November IPO pricing, not OpenAI's headlines. The first public price either lab gets will shape API pricing and model availability for both.
  • If you are an investor: remember the $1.25 trillion is a private price, set by funds with their own interests. The public price, when it comes, can be lower.
  • If you are a student: the labs are hiring, and the skills that matter are the ones that survive a public market: safety, evaluation, infrastructure, and shipping real products.
  • If you just use ChatGPT: nothing changes tomorrow. But the company behind it is about to have public shareholders, and public shareholders want growth, not patience.

The real pattern

OpenAI is worth $1.25 trillion in private and will not go public because of safety, while Anthropic is going public first with $16.3 billion of revenue and $750 billion of compute promises. The pattern is not that one lab is careful and the other is bold. The pattern is that the AI industry has become too big to stay private and too risky to go public, and both labs are trying to cross that gap without falling in. Whoever sets the first public price wins the argument about what all of this is actually worth.

Final thoughts

A trillion dollars is a number so big it stops meaning anything, and that is exactly the point. OpenAI is worth $1.25 trillion because a small group of people agreed it is, and the rest of us will find out whether they were right when the public market finally gets its say. Until then, remember who set the price, and who was not in the room.
