The 9 Things Jensen Huang Leaving Nvidia Proves in 2026, and Why AI Just Lost Its Most Important CEO

The man who built the AI chip empire is gone, and the company that powers every model you use has no permanent leader.

Jensen Huang on stage in his signature black leather jacket. The keynote era is over. Photo: Forbes

Let's stop pretending a CEO resignation is just a personnel story.

Here is the uncomfortable truth: one man was the single point of failure for the entire AI industry, and on Thursday, November 12, he walked out the door.

This article is not a eulogy for Jensen Huang. It is about what happens when the company that sells the picks and shovels of the AI gold rush loses the man who decided who gets the shovels.

1. The board fired him without using the word fired

Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive, president, and board member of Nvidia, effective immediately. He co-founded the company in 1993, so this was 33 years ending in a single morning.

  • The board statement never mentioned the scandal or the personal conduct revelations at all
  • It called his contributions "profound" and thanked him for his "immeasurable contributions"
  • The board had been set to oust him just before the resignation was announced, and it had already hired Wachtell Lipton to run an independent investigation

Boards do not accept instant resignations from founder CEOs unless the alternative is worse.

(Source: The Guardian)

2. The market did the maths before the press release finished loading

Wall Street does not wait for the think pieces. It priced in the key man risk in minutes.

  • Shares plunged 13% when trading opened after the departure was announced
  • They closed down 4.3% on Thursday, wiping more than $180 billion off Nvidia's value in a single day
  • About $490 billion vanished across the week, leaving the company valued at $4 trillion, after its market cap hit $5 trillion last year

A $180 billion haircut in one day is the market saying the CEO was the moat.

(Source: The Guardian)

3. One man had 60 direct reports

Huang ran Nvidia the way founders run companies when nobody ever tells them no.

  • He managed roughly 60 direct reports, a span of control no management textbook would bless
  • There was no obvious number two, no heir apparent groomed in public
  • The interim replacement is the CFO, who is keeping her finance job at the same time

The org chart was a star, not a pyramid, and the star just left.

(Source: The Guardian)

4. The interim CEO is the CFO, and that tells you everything

Colette Kress, Nvidia's chief financial officer for 13 years, is now interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent successor. She keeps the CFO job in the meantime.

  • The board said it will consider internal and external candidates
  • Harvey Jones, a tech investor and entrepreneur, was named Nvidia's first ever board chair
  • Kress wrote to staff that the period has been "extraordinarily difficult", and the company will hold a staff meeting on November 18

This is a steady hands pick, not a vision pick. The board bought time.

Huang presenting with an Nvidia chip graphic behind him. The product roadmap now belongs to someone else. Photo: Magazyn T3

(Source: The Guardian, Business Times)

5. The succession list reads like a fantasy draft

When the most important CEO job in tech opens up, everyone has a name. Almost none of them are realistic.

  • Donald Trump posted that Tim Cook should replace Huang, writing "A Legend replaces a Legend!" Cook stepped down as Apple chief executive in September but remains executive chair
  • Rene Haas, chief executive of Arm Holdings, and Lisa Su, chief executive of AMD, are the other outside names in circulation
  • Internal candidates include Kress and Ian Buck, who runs Nvidia's AI hardware and software products
  • Huang's children Madison and Spencer work at Nvidia, but they are in their mid-30s and relatively inexperienced

Every name on the list is either unavailable, a rival CEO, or unproven at this scale.

(Source: The Guardian)

6. Congress is not done with him just because he quit

Resigning ends the job. It does not end the inquiry.

  • Huang has agreed to testify before Congress before Thanksgiving, in a closed-door session
  • The request came in a November 4 letter from three Republican committee chairs: John Moolenaar, Brian Mast, and Rick Crawford, who proposed November 18
  • Lawmakers were prepared to seek a subpoena if no date could be agreed
  • They want to know whether Chinese intelligence gained access to anything during the affair, and what it obtained, plus how Huang lobbied on US restrictions for advanced chip exports

Quitting the company does not quit the questions.

(Source: Economic Times, Newsmax)

7. The Democrats opened a second front

Congress is not the only investigation. It is not even the only party investigating.

  • Democrats in Congress launched an investigation into Nvidia's China sales and its dealings with Trump
  • This is the second such investigation into the company
  • Top Democrat Gregory Meeks is preparing a subcommittee to examine the fallout in the next Congress, saying "Come Jan. 4, there will be some hearings"

This is now a bipartisan, multi-committee story, and it will outlive the news cycle.

(Source: TechCrunch)

8. The affair is confirmed. The spying is not.

This is the line that matters most, so let me be exact about it.

  • On Sunday, Huang admitted he had been unfaithful to his wife, Lori, without naming anyone directly
  • Alice Huang, no relation, is a businesswoman who headed the Singapore datacentre firm Megaspeed. US intelligence services suspect her of being a Chinese asset, according to multiple reports. Huang has denied knowing she allegedly worked for the Chinese government
  • Late on Wednesday, the New York Times reported a separate affair with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice-president of healthcare, reportedly captured on video being affectionate with Huang at a hotel during a company conference in March
  • After the resignation, Reuters reported a third liaison with another Nvidia employee, according to two people familiar with the matter

A confirmed affair is not a confirmed espionage case. Conflating the two is exactly how a scandal becomes a witch hunt.

Nvidia's chips train the models and power the companies behind them. The hardware does not resign. Image: Notebookcheck

(Source: The Guardian)

9. Every AI model you use runs on his chips

Zoom out from the scandal for a second and look at the machine underneath it.

  • Nvidia's chips train AI models and power companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic
  • The AI arms race between the US and China is at its apex, and Nvidia sits in the middle of it
  • Analyst Dan Ives said the board had done what it needed to do, including to "rip off the Band-Aid", and that the company remained ahead of competitors
  • Analyst Neil Wilson said Nvidia would need a "great CEO", adding that Huang's technical knowledge was "unique and impossible to replicate"
  • Nvidia reports third-quarter earnings on Tuesday, days after losing its founder

A $4 trillion company with no permanent CEO, reporting earnings in days, while the whole industry waits on its chips.

(Source: The Guardian, Financial Express)

How to start

Three reader types, three things to watch.

  • If you build on Nvidia: watch the earnings call on Tuesday and the succession pick, not the scandal headlines. Your GPU supply and your CUDA roadmap do not change this week.
  • If you invest in AI: the succession pick matters more than the quarter. A caretaker CFO plus a slow search is priced in now. A surprise name is not.
  • If you just use AI tools: notice how much of your daily work depends on one company's chips and one man's decisions. That is the story underneath the scandal.

Do not panic-migrate anything. Do pay attention to who gets the job.

The real pattern

The real pattern is not that a CEO fell. It is that the entire AI boom was built on a company that was, in the end, one man deep, and the industry spent a decade pretending that was a strength. Sixty direct reports, no heir, a $5 trillion valuation resting on one leather jacket. The scandal was the trigger. The concentration was the condition.

Final thoughts

Founders are not immortal, and empires built on one person are not companies, they are bets. Nvidia's chips are still the best in the world. The question now is whether the company can be bigger than the man who made it. We find out on Tuesday, and then again whenever the board names his replacement.
