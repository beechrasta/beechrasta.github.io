The 9 Things Amazon and Google Owning Nearly 30 Percent of Anthropic Proves in 2026, and Why the Independent AI Lab Is Dead

Anthropic prices its IPO tonight. The founders kept the votes, and the cloud giants kept everything else.

Amazon's Spheres in Seattle. The cloud giant is about to own nearly 15 percent of the company that makes Claude. Photo: Gizmodo

Let's stop pretending Anthropic is an independent AI lab.

Amazon and Google will own nearly 30 percent of the company after its IPO, and Amazon alone could have owned almost a quarter.

This article is not a list of IPO numbers. It is about who actually owns the AI future, and it is not the people building the models.

1. The number: 28.4 percent, and none of it votes

Anthropic's amended S-1 filing, filed November 10 and updated November 17, finally put hard numbers on the worst-kept secret in AI: the cloud giants own the lab.

  • Amazon will hold 14.7 percent of Anthropic after the IPO and a concurrent private placement
  • Google will hold 13.7 percent, taking the combined stake to 28.4 percent
  • Both stakes are held in Class N stock, which carries no voting rights. Amazon will own 33.9 percent of that share class, Google 31.6 percent

Nearly a third of the company, zero percent of the vote. That split is the whole story.

(Source: TechCrunch)

2. Amazon could have owned 23.8 percent and said no

Here is the detail that tells you how deliberate this is. Amazon could have owned much more, and it chose not to.

  • When Amazon invested billions in Anthropic in 2023 and 2024, back when Claude looked like a speck in OpenAI's shadow, it received promissory notes convertible into equity
  • Those notes convert at about $2.09 or $5.05 per share, against an IPO price of $70 to $78
  • Amazon converted only part of them, and the filing says it "has elected not to waive" an ownership cap. If it converted everything today, it would own 23.8 percent, worth roughly $272 billion at the top of the price range

Amazon left billions of dollars of equity on the table, on purpose. The notes stay outstanding, the exposure stays, and the headline stake stays under 15 percent.

(Source: TechCrunch)

3. The founders kept the votes anyway

If the cloud giants own the economics, who owns the company? The seven co-founders, and it is not close.

  • The co-founders will hold 12.8 percent of the shares, and 61.3 percent of the voting power
  • Amazon, Google and the Public Benefit Fund together hold 34.8 percent of the shares and 0 percent of the general vote
  • Add the founders to the two clouds and three holders account for 41.1 percent of all Anthropic shares

This is the deal the labs always wanted: the founders keep control, the clouds keep the returns, and the public gets the ticker.

(Source: FourWeekMBA, from Anthropic's S-1/A of 10 November 2026)

4. Amazon's billions became a nine-figure fortune

Do the math on Amazon's side of the trade, because it is the best investment of the decade and nobody is talking about it that way.

  • Amazon's stake of 14.7 percent is worth roughly $135 billion to $150 billion at the $70 to $78 price range
  • That stake is about 4.8 to 5.4 percent of Amazon's own market value, on a market capitalization of about $2.79 trillion
  • Each $1 move in Anthropic's stock is worth about $1.9 billion to Amazon and $1.8 billion to Google

Amazon bought a rounding error and turned it into a top-ten holding of itself.

(Source: Asianet Newsable, from the amended S-1)

The Googleplex in Mountain View. Google's 13.7 percent stake makes it the second-largest owner of the Claude maker. Photo: Tecnoblog

5. Anthropic's paper gains are nearly half of Amazon's profit

This is the number that made me sit up. Anthropic is not just an investment on Amazon's balance sheet, it is currently a large chunk of Amazon's profits.

  • Upward adjustments to Amazon's Anthropic preferred stock made up about $62.8 billion, or 42.6 percent of Amazon's pretax income in the first nine months of 2026, by one analysis of Amazon's 10-Q
  • That is paper value, not cash, and it moves with Anthropic's valuation, which means Amazon's earnings now move with a company it does not control
  • The IPO turns that paper into a market price, which is why the listing matters to Amazon's shareholders as much as to Anthropic's

Nearly half of Amazon's pretax profit this year came from owning a piece of an AI lab. The cloud business is still the business, but the AI stake is now the earnings story.

(Source: FourWeekMBA, from Amazon's 10-Q)

6. Every chipmaker bought a ticket

The IPO is not just Amazon and Google. Seven of the biggest technology companies on earth agreed to buy $26 billion of Anthropic's non-voting stock in a concurrent private placement.

  • The buyers: AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC Partners
  • Google and Nvidia are each taking $10 billion worth
  • Anthropic's existing backers Sequoia and Lightspeed are also looking to invest more in the IPO, according to Bloomberg

When your suppliers, your customers and your competitors all buy your stock on the same day, you are not a company anymore. You are infrastructure.

(Source: TechCrunch)

7. The compute bill: roughly 900 billion dollars

The filing also disclosed what Anthropic owes its owners for the privilege of existing, and the number is staggering.

  • Anthropic's total committed spending comes to roughly $900.9 billion, by a Yahoo Finance review of the amended S-1
  • Minimum purchase obligations of at least $110 billion for Amazon compute, and at least $121.3 billion for Google compute between April 2026 and July 2033
  • Another $47.2 billion to $49.2 billion to Microsoft between November 2026 and June 2033
  • The Google commitment is paid whether or not the capacity is used

Anthropic's owners sell it the compute, take equity in return, and collect a guaranteed nine-figure bill for a decade. That is not a partnership. That is a toll road.

(Source: Yahoo Finance, via Ava Fund)

8. Six times oversubscribed, pricing tonight

The market, for its part, does not care about any of this. It wants in.

  • Orders for the IPO reached about six times the shares on offer, according to Bloomberg
  • Anthropic is set to price the offering on November 18 at $70 to $78 a share, and the Wall Street Journal reports it could price above $80, lifting the valuation past $1.5 trillion
  • The offering covers 825 million Class A shares, with net proceeds of about $84.6 billion, or about $93.7 billion if the underwriters take their option in full
  • Trading starts the next day on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT

A $1.5 trillion valuation for a company whose two biggest shareholders are its own suppliers. The market is not buying a lab. It is buying the toll road.

(Source: Ava Fund, from Bloomberg and the Wall Street Journal)

The New York Stock Exchange trading floor. Anthropic lists on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT. Photo: Forbes

9. Your cloud provider owns your AI lab

Zoom out, and Anthropic is not an exception. It is the pattern, and the pattern now covers the whole frontier.

  • OpenAI is bound to Microsoft by a multi-billion-dollar investment and a compute deal that runs to hundreds of billions
  • Anthropic is bound to Amazon and Google by equity, convertible notes and a $900 billion compute bill
  • The labs needed compute no startup could afford, the clouds needed models no cloud could build, and the deal was always going to be equity for electricity

Independence was a funding stage, not a business model. The IPO just puts a price on the end of it.

(Source: TechCrunch)

How to start

  • If you invest: watch the pricing tonight and the first trade tomorrow under ANT. The range is $70 to $78, the whisper is above $80, and the order book is six times covered. Do not chase the open.
  • If you build on Claude: the product does not change, the incentives do. Your model provider is now a public company whose largest shareholders are its own compute suppliers. That is worth knowing before you bet a product on it.
  • If you just watch the industry: the number to remember is not $1.5 trillion, it is 28.4 percent. Every future AI lab will be measured against it.
  • If you are an Indian reader: the same playbook is coming to your market, global clouds buying into local AI champions. Ask who owns the lab before you trust the lab.

The real pattern

The AI labs were never going to stay independent. Training a frontier model costs more than any startup can raise, so the labs sold the only thing they had, equity, to the only buyers who had what they needed, compute. Anthropic's IPO does not end that deal. It prices it. The independent AI lab was a funding stage, not a business model.

Final thoughts

Anthropic goes public tonight as a $1.5 trillion company that two of its biggest customers already own nearly a third of. The founders kept the votes, the clouds kept the economics, and the rest of us got a ticker symbol. ANT starts trading tomorrow. The lab is public. The ownership was settled years ago.
