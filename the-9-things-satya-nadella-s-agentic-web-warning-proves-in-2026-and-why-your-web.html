The 9 Things Satya Nadella's Agentic Web Warning Proves in 2026, and Why Your Website Just Lost Its Audience

The Microsoft CEO just told the world's biggest companies that the web's business model is about to be reshuffled. Amazon already chose its side.

Microsoft's office in Boston. Its CEO just warned that the companies monetizing human eyeballs are about to get reshuffled. Photo: Praswin Prakashan on Unsplash

Let's stop pretending AI agents are a feature you can add to your website later.

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella stood on stage at the New York Times DealBook Summit on November 18 and said the rise of AI agents is going to reshape the economics of the internet. Not someday. Now, while Amazon is blocking agents and Walmart is welcoming them.

This article is not about a prediction. It is about a split that already happened, and which side of it your favorite website chose.

1. The quote that matters: "reshuffled"

Nadella's warning was one sentence, and it was aimed at every company that makes money from people visiting websites. "The current aggregators who have a business model which was for the human web will get, in some sense, reshuffled," he said.

  • If you were monetizing ads because of the eyeballs, he continued, you now have to probably get down to essentially maybe CPC, cost-per-click, wins, or maybe something more transactional
  • The shift to an agentic web could change which companies pay others for traffic and for transactions, the same way the move from web portals like Yahoo to search engines like Google did years ago
  • The same week, Nadella was also explaining Microsoft's voluntary buyout program covering around 60,000 employees on The Pragmatic Engineer podcast, so he is reshaping his own company while warning everyone else

When the CEO of Microsoft says your business model is about to be reshuffled, he is not guessing. He is describing his own calendar.

(Source: TechCrunch)

2. The human web is the product being disrupted

Nadella's phrase "human web" is doing a lot of work. It means the internet as we know it: humans browsing, clicking, scrolling, seeing ads, and occasionally buying something. Agents break every step of that chain.

  • An agent does not see your banner ad, does not scroll past your sponsored post, and does not linger on your homepage
  • An agent compares prices across five stores in seconds, buys the cheapest one, and never once loads the page your advertiser paid for
  • If the agent does the browsing, the ad impression never happens, the click never happens, and the "eyeballs" the whole model is priced on were never there

The web's business model assumed a human was looking. The agent is not looking, it is buying.

(Source: TradersUnion)

3. Yahoo to Google, the last reshuffle, and who got paid

Nadella compared the coming shift to the move from web portals to search engines, and the comparison is worth taking seriously, because that reshuffle has a body count.

  • Portals like Yahoo owned the front door of the internet, then Google replaced the front door with a search box, and the money followed the search box
  • The portals did not die because their pages got worse. They died because the way people arrived changed, and the new arrival method had a different toll booth
  • Agents are the new arrival method. They arrive, they compare, they transact, and they do not stop at the toll booth on the way in

Every reshuffle has winners who saw it coming and losers who loved the old model too much to leave it.

(Source: TechCrunch)

4. Amazon slammed the door

The discussion at DealBook turned to Amazon, and Amazon is the clearest example of a company choosing to fight the reshuffle. On September 20, Amazon blocked Meta's Muse agent from shopping on Amazon.com.

  • Muse users trying to shop on Amazon got a pop-up: "Continued access by an unauthorized AI agent violates Amazon's Conditions of Use, to which our customers have agreed"
  • Amazon's complaints were threefold: Muse did not identify itself as an agent, Meta never told Amazon the agent would shop there, and the agent appeared to capture and store customer credentials
  • Meta disputes the credential point, saying Muse has no visibility into people's passwords or payment methods, and neither side has published technical proof

Amazon would rather lose the agent than lose the customer relationship, and it wrote that preference into its terms of use.

(Source: Digital Matters)

5. Walmart opened a different door

The same story has a second character, and it chose the opposite strategy. Walmart announced its Agent Gateway and Agent Profiles on October 21, letting approved agents search inventory and make purchases through a structured connection.

  • The gateway is designed for agents to shop through a proper connection, rather than navigating the retailer's site as a person would
  • Agent Profiles give customers controls such as spending limits, so the agent shops inside rules the customer set
  • Amazon also runs its own agents, its Buy for Me feature shops on outside brands' sites, and Amazon says its agent identifies itself and lets brands opt out

The same week Amazon built a wall, Walmart built a gate. Only one of those strategies scales to a world full of agents.

(Source: Great Money)

A shopper paying on a phone with a card in hand. Agents do this step without the human, which is exactly why the stores are fighting over them. Photo: Michael lima on Unsplash

6. The blockade is spreading beyond retail

Amazon is not alone, and that is the part nobody should miss. CNBC reported on November 1 that Amazon, JPMorgan Chase, Charles Schwab, Kohl's, and Neiman Marcus had all moved to block AI agents acting for customers in the prior six weeks.

  • Amazon had already moved to restrict shopping agents from Google and OpenAI before the Muse fight, and it sued Perplexity over its Comet browser on November 4, 2025
  • Cloudflare reorganized its AI controls on September 15 into three groups, Search, Training, and Agent, and its Agent control lets sites allow agents, block them on pages with ads, or block them entirely
  • On Amazon's October 29 earnings call, CEO Andy Jassy said few customers shop through outside agents because the experience is not great or more convenient than visiting a store directly

Blocking agents is now a corporate policy, not a bug, and the blockers include the biggest store and the biggest banks in America.

(Source: S and P C Zone)

7. Shopify's opening, and Nadella's bluntest line

Nadella's bluntest line at DealBook was not about Amazon. It was about everyone watching Amazon. "And so if you don't accept the agent, somebody else will," he said.

  • His example was Shopify: if Amazon keeps blocking agents, another shopping platform such as Shopify could allow them and gain an advantage
  • "I think this is going to be interesting," he said. "It's the same thing that's happening in other categories, right? Which is, if you're in love with a particular business model, you have to kind of scramble"
  • The logic is simple and brutal. The agent goes where it is allowed, and the sale follows the agent

Openness is now a competitive weapon, and the closed door is an invitation to your rival.

(Source: TechCrunch)

8. The numbers behind the agent are already real

This is not a forecast about a future nobody can measure. Meta launched Muse on September 8, 2026, and the numbers from its first six weeks are the reason every CEO is suddenly talking about agents.

  • More than 10 million people in the United States and Canada had set up Muse in roughly six weeks, according to Meta's October 21 earnings call
  • Shopping was its most common use, and the average purchase was above $200, which makes the agent a serious customer, not a toy
  • These are Meta's own reported figures, not independently verified usage data, but the direction is unmistakable: agents already buy things, at real prices, in real volume

Agents are not a demo. They are already the checkout, and the checkout is where the money changes hands.

(Source: Great Money)

9. What it means for anyone who makes things for the web

Here is where the reshuffle stops being a CEO panel topic and starts being your problem. If agents do the browsing, the whole chain of who gets paid changes, and it changes for everyone at once.

  • Publishers: if an agent reads your article and summarizes it, your traffic falls and your ad inventory falls with it, so the "free content, paid by ads" model starts to wobble
  • Advertisers: eyeball pricing gives way to cost-per-click and transactional models, which means paying for outcomes instead of attention
  • Small sellers: whoever lets agents in gets the sale, and whoever blocks them gets skipped by the machine doing the comparing
  • Builders and developers in India and everywhere else: the next SEO is being agent-friendly, structured data, clear prices, and a front door an agent is allowed to walk through

The next SEO is not ranking for humans. It is being the store the agent is allowed to enter.

(Source: TechCrunch)

A person browsing a website on a laptop in a cafe. This is the human web Nadella is talking about, the one agents are about to replace as the default visitor. Photo: Levlan

How to start

Pick your side of the split, because the split is already here.

  • Builders: expose structured endpoints, keep prices machine-readable, and give agents a front door instead of a CAPTCHA wall. The agent that cannot enter your site will buy from the one that can.
  • Sellers: watch the Shopify and Walmart agent gateways, and set spending limits and rules before your customers' agents set them for you.
  • Readers: your agent will soon do the browsing and the buying. Learn its spending limits now, the same way you learned your card's credit limit.

You don't need to predict the winner. You need to be reachable by the agent, whatever it is called.

The real pattern

The web is splitting into two webs, one for humans and one for agents, and the money is following the agents. The human web was built on attention, and attention was always a proxy for intent. Agents carry the intent directly, with a wallet attached, so the proxy is no longer needed. Every company now has to decide whether it sells to the person or to the person's agent, and the ones that refuse to choose will discover the agent chose for them.

Final thoughts

Yahoo loved the portal, and Google loved the search box, and both were right until the arrival method changed. The arrival method is changing again, and this time the visitor is software with a budget. Build for the visitor that is coming, not the one that is leaving. The web will still be there. The question is who it is built for.
