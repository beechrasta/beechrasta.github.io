The 9 Things SpaceX Becoming a Computer Landlord Proves in 2026, and Why Musk's Biggest Customer Is the Rival He Cannot Beat

The rocket company now makes more money renting out GPUs than launching rockets. Its best customer is the AI lab trying to beat its own chatbot.

The Colossus supercomputer campus in Memphis, Tennessee. Image: CodedTrip

Let's stop pretending SpaceX is a rocket company. According to the New York Times, its AI unit now brings in more than half the company's revenue, and it earns that money by renting computing power to the very rivals Elon Musk is trying to beat.

SpaceXSI, the AI division, made 7.56 billion dollars in the third quarter of 2026. That is about 52 percent of SpaceX's total revenue of 14.58 billion dollars, up 161 percent from a year earlier.

This article is not about rockets. It is about the day the world's most famous rocket company quietly became a landlord.

1. The rocket company is now a landlord

  • SpaceX's AI segment out-earned both its satellite connectivity business and its space operations in the third quarter.
  • The money does not come from Grok, the chatbot. It comes from leasing spare GPU capacity to outside AI labs, mostly Anthropic and Google.
  • The engine is Colossus, the data center operation in Memphis, Tennessee. Colossus 1 alone houses more than 220,000 Nvidia GPUs.

SpaceX built its reputation on rockets and satellites. Its biggest revenue line is now rented GPUs.

(Source: Crypto Briefing)

2. Anthropic pays the rent, and the rent is absurd

  • Anthropic agreed in May 2026 to use the full capacity of Colossus 1, with Colossus 2 capacity included in the arrangement.
  • Once all contracted capacity is delivered, expected in December, Anthropic will pay about 3.1 billion dollars a month. The agreements run to 120.5 billion dollars through 2029.
  • In the third quarter alone, Anthropic supplied about 34.5 percent of SpaceX's total revenue, roughly 5.03 billion dollars of 14.575 billion, making it SpaceX's biggest customer, ahead of even the US government.

One customer, one third of the entire company. That is not a client. That is a lifeline.

(Source: TechTimes)

3. Google is the second tenant, and the numbers do not quite agree

  • Google's contract was disclosed on June 5, 2026. The filing puts the fee at 920 million dollars a month from October 2026 through June 2029, for roughly 110,000 Nvidia GPUs.
  • The New York Times reports the figure as about 800 million dollars a month, a gap that probably reflects the ramp-up period or rounding. Either way, it is close to a billion a month.
  • Add the two deals plus smaller contracts, and SpaceX's annualised revenue from compute leasing could top 26 billion dollars, with total contracted value above 70 billion dollars.

When your second-biggest tenant pays a billion a month and the reports cannot even agree on the exact number, the money has stopped being the interesting part. The dependence is.

(Source: Durov's Code)

4. The group chat rename that said the quiet part out loud

  • In August, Musk temporarily renamed a group chat of hundreds of SpaceX AI engineers to "Sell Compute Because We Can't Compete".
  • His message to the engineers, quoted by the Times: "At the end of the day, the only thing that matters for competitiveness is revenue per GPU, not benchmarks. If you can't pay for your GPUs, then you either go bankrupt or you become a computer rental company."
  • He told employees SpaceX is falling behind in AI development and demanded greater accountability for how its expensive GPUs are used.

Musk did not spin this. He named the group chat after the surrender and made hundreds of engineers read it every day.

(Source: The Outpost)

5. Revenue per GPU is the new benchmark

  • Under Musk's rule, a GPU hour spent on Grok has to beat roughly what Anthropic pays for the same hardware, about 5 dollars per GPU hour on the first contract.
  • That turns every internal AI project into a tenant competing with paying customers for the same chips.
  • Morningstar analyst Nicolas Owens told the Times that computing rentals bring in substantially more money than Grok itself.

Benchmarks are for press releases. Revenue per GPU is for the landlord.

(Source: TechTimes)

Inside the Colossus hall in Memphis. Image: SpaceX

6. Grok is the tenant who got evicted from his own building

  • Internal documents cited by the Times show xAI used less than half of the Colossus data center capacity last year, which is why so much of it was available to rent out in the first place.
  • SpaceX moved money away from Digital Optimus, a Tesla-linked project to train AI models on video games, and tightened control over its own developers' GPU use.
  • On November 13, SpaceX laid off about 60 people from Grok Imagine, its image and video generator, leaving a handful to maintain it.

The rocket company's own AI is the side project now. The paying customers are the main business.

(Source: Business Report)

7. The 90-day clause that could end the whole party

  • Either side can walk away from the Anthropic deal with 90 days' notice, after initial periods ending in January and March 2027.
  • The 120.5 billion dollar ceiling is not guaranteed revenue. It is a maximum, and Anthropic is estimated to have contributed a third of SpaceX's quarterly revenue already.
  • Musk himself said at the DealBook Summit on November 18 that Anthropic could want another 10 gigawatts of capacity, and that it is extremely likely the company will keep renting. He is publicly cheering for the tenant to stay.

A landlord with one tenant paying a third of the rent and a 90-day exit clause is not rich. He is one phone call away from a very bad quarter.

(Source: Crypto Briefing)

8. A million data centers in space, because Earth ran out of room

  • On November 25, the Federal Communications Commission approved SpaceX launching up to a million data centers in space, a plan Musk has pushed as land-based data centers face populist backlash over their resource demands.
  • Back on Earth, SpaceXSI built a new Memphis data center to fill orders, completed in 122 days, which the Times calls the largest in the industry.
  • Meanwhile SpaceXSI president Mike Nicolls told staff this month that the unit is falling behind Anthropic, the same Anthropic paying it billions.

The company that cannot beat its tenant on Earth is now planning to put the data centers in orbit. That is either genius or the most expensive change of subject in history.

(Source: AOL)

A SpaceX Starship launch. Image: 7minutos.es

9. What this means for everyone who is not Elon Musk

  • For AI labs: the compute market has a new landlord, and the landlord's prices set the floor for everyone. If Anthropic is paying 5 dollars a GPU hour, your startup's GPU bill just got a benchmark.
  • For developers: the fastest-growing infrastructure business in AI is not a cloud provider. It is a rocket company that realised renting beats building. Expect more weird landlords.
  • For the AI race itself: the most honest sentence in this whole story is Musk's own. If you cannot pay for your GPUs, you either go bankrupt or you become a computer rental company. SpaceX chose the rental company, and it is making billions.

The AI race is not being won by the best model. Right now it is being funded by the best landlord.

(Source: SE Daily)

How to start

  • If you are a developer: watch GPU rental prices the way you watch model benchmarks. The Colossus deals set a public price floor, about 5 dollars a GPU hour, and that number decides which of your projects is worth running.
  • If you are a founder: the lesson is not "build a data center." It is "find the asset you already own that someone else will pay rent for." SpaceX owned idle GPUs. You own something idle too.
  • If you are an investor: a rocket company where AI rentals are 52 percent of revenue is not a space stock anymore. Price it like infrastructure, and watch the 90-day clause the way you would watch a single-customer risk.

The real pattern

Every AI company is really two companies: the one that builds models and the one that pays for the electricity. SpaceX just admitted, in a group chat name and a quarterly filing, that the second company is the better business. The AI race is not a race to the smartest model. It is a race to whoever can afford to keep the lights on, and right now the landlord is winning.

Final thoughts

I write about Meta Muse and Petra directly because they run on the same economics: someone, somewhere, is paying for the GPUs that answer you.

Musk sells rockets, satellites, and now computing power to his own rivals. The most Musk sentence ever written might be the one in that group chat: if you cannot pay for your GPUs, you become a computer rental company.

He became one. And he is making billions doing it.
